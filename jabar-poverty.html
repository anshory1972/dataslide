Part 2 · West Java on the World Bank income scale

By GRDP per capita, West Java is in the lower-middle-income range; six of its 27 districts are in the upper-middle range

Source & method
Countries: GNI per capita, Atlas method, 2025 (2024 where 2025 is missing), World Bank WDI, and the FY2027 thresholds (US$1,175; 4,635; 14,375). Provinces and districts: BPS GRDP per capita at current prices, 2025, multiplied by Indonesia's ratio of GNI per capita (Atlas, US$5,120) to GDP per capita in rupiah (Rp83.4 million), that is Rp16,284 per US$. This is an approximation by the author, not a World Bank classification: GRDP measures production in a district, not the income of its residents. 6 districts are above US$4,635 and 21 are between US$1,176 and 4,635. Provinces on 2012 boundaries (33).

Part 4 · Poverty at higher lines

In West Java, poverty is 6.5% at the national line, 13.2% at the $4.20 line and 58.6% at the $8.30 line

Source & method
Susenas March 2026, weighted by persons; author's calculation. Spending per person is converted to national prices: spending × national poverty line ÷ the poverty line of the household's own province and urban or rural area. Lines per month: national Rp669,235; $4.20 = Rp786,301; $8.30 = Rp1,553,881 (2021 PPP, Rp6,155 per $). The World Bank adjusts prices by district, so its figures differ: for March 2025 this method gives 17.9% and 64.8% for Indonesia, against the World Bank's 15.5% and 64.1%.

Part 6 · Convergence

From 2015 to 2025, spending grew faster, and poverty and the Gini ratio fell more, in districts that started from a worse position

Source & method
Susenas March 2015 and March 2025, author's calculation; official district poverty rates from BPS (485 districts with the same code in both years; 34 provinces on 2015 boundaries, province poverty computed from Susenas and equal to BPS). Mean spending 2015 is divided by the province urban or rural poverty line. Real growth uses the national CPI (March 2025 / March 2015 = 1.343), so price differences between regions over time are not removed. Lines: least-squares fits for districts (solid) and provinces (dashed). A negative slope can also come from sampling error in the 2015 values.

Part 7 · Sector growth and poverty

Manufacturing and modern services are the sectors whose growth goes with falling district poverty; non-modern services and agriculture show no link

Source & method
Districts of Indonesia, official BPS poverty rates. Dependent variable: annual change in the log of the poverty rate × 100. Sector terms are contributions to GRDP growth in points a year (sector change divided by total GRDP at the start), so they add up to total growth. Three designs: one observation per district for 2015–2025 (475 districts); five 2-year periods pooled (2,488 observations) with period effects and initial poverty; the same with district effects. The top and bottom 1% of GRDP growth are dropped; standard errors clustered by district. GRDP at 2010 prices by 17 sectors: BPS, compiled and corrected in Wardhana, Yusuf and Anna (2026). Modern services: information, finance, real estate, business services. Non-modern services: trade, transport, accommodation and food, public administration, education, health, other services. These are associations, not causal effects.

Part 8 · Jobs: unemployment and formal work

Across West Java districts, poverty has no relation with unemployment (r = −0.19) and a strong relation with formal work (r = −0.70)

Source & method
27 districts. Poverty: BPS official district rate, March 2024. Unemployment and formal share: Sakernas August 2024, author's calculation with the definitions of Wardhana, Yusuf and Anna (2026). Formal: employee, or employer with permanent workers (main job). The unemployment rate here is 5.4% for Indonesia against 4.9% published by BPS. Across all districts of Indonesia the correlations are −0.22 for unemployment and −0.55 for formal work.

Part 9 · Falling formal employment in West Java

The formal share of workers in West Java fell from 49.6% in 2015 to 45.6% in 2024; it rose in Central and East Java

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015, 2019 and 2024, workers aged 15 and over; author's calculation. Formal: main-job status is employee, or employer with permanent workers. This is the status-based definition used in BPS labour releases; it does not measure contracts or social insurance. For Indonesia it gives 42.0% in 2024.

Part 9 · Falling formal employment in West Java

Manufacturing accounts for 3.2 of the 4.0-point fall: it added 634,000 workers, of whom 136,000 were formal

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, West Java, workers aged 15 and over; author's calculation. Each bar is the change in the sector's formal share weighted by its share of workers, plus the effect of its changing share of workers. The bars add up to the total change. Sector codes for 2019 have known errors and are not used. West Java added 5.6 million workers in the period; 1.8 million were formal.

Part 9 · Falling formal employment in West Java

The fall is among women (47% to 38%), workers aged 15–24 (72% to 62%) and urban areas (57% to 50%)

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, West Java; author's calculation. Women were 31.5% of workers in 2015 and 37.3% in 2024. By employment status, the share of employees fell from 46.2% to 42.3%; own-account workers rose by 1.87 million and employees by 1.65 million.

Part 9 · Falling formal employment in West Java

The eight districts with the highest formal share added 1.3 million workers and 117,000 formal workers; the other 19 added 4.3 million and 1.7 million

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, by district of residence; author's calculation. The eight districts had a formal share of 55% or more in 2015: Kota Depok, Kota Bogor, Kota Bekasi, Kab. Bekasi, Kota Bandung, Kota Cimahi, Kota Cirebon and Kab. Bogor. Formal manufacturing workers fell by 251,000 in these eight and rose by 387,000 in the other 19. District figures come from samples of a few hundred workers and have wide margins; the two group totals are more reliable.

Part 10 · Where manufacturing work went

Central Java added 637,000 formal manufacturing workers and West Java 136,000, while West Java kept its share of output

Source & method
Workers: Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, by province of residence; author's calculation. Output: manufacturing value added at 2010 prices, BPS district GRDP summed to provinces. The two together suggest that labour-intensive manufacturing moved while capital-intensive manufacturing stayed; there are no district data by sub-industry to confirm this.

Part 10 · Where manufacturing work went

The minimum wage in Kab. Bekasi is 2.5 times that of Brebes, as in 2015; in rupiah the difference grew from Rp1.7 million to Rp3.1 million a month

Source & method
Red: industrial districts of West Java. Blue: north-east West Java. Green: Central Java. West Java: Kepgub 560/Kep.1581-Bangsos/2014 (Kontan, 22 Nov 2014), 2024 list (CNBC Indonesia, 1 Dec 2023), Kepgub 561.7/Kep.798-Kesra/2024, Kepgub 561/Kep.862-Kesra/2025. Central Java: Kepgub 560/85/2014 (Liputan6, 24 Nov 2014); 2024-2026 BPS Jawa Tengah table 708. The West Java figures are from published lists of the decrees, not from the decree annexes. In West Java, 24% of poor households receive none of five programmes, against 18% in Indonesia; 16% of the bottom 40% receive three or more

Source & method
Susenas March 2025; author's calculation. PIP and PBI JKN count a household if any member has them. Part 12 · Summary

Three directions for West Java, with the evidence for each

For West JavaEvidence in this deck
1. Reindustrialisation, with wages set in line with labour productivityManufacturing growth goes with falling district poverty. West Java's formal share fell from 49.6% to 45.6%; manufacturing explains 3.2 of the 4.0 points. Formal manufacturing workers: +136,000 in West Java, +637,000 in Central Java. Minimum wage in Kab. Bekasi: 2.5 times that of Brebes.
2. Modern servicesThe sector with the strongest link to poverty reduction in 2-year periods, about three times the manufacturing effect at the $4.20 and $8.30 lines. It has 5% of West Java workers and 11% of output. Non-modern services has 52% of workers and no link.
3. Social protection that reaches the poor7.0 million people are below the $4.20 line and 30.8 million below $8.30. 24% of poor households receive none of five programmes; 55% of Kartu Sembako recipients are outside the bottom 40%.
