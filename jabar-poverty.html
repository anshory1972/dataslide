Part 4 · Poverty at higher lines

West Java poverty: 6.5% at the national line, 13.2% at $4.20, 58.6% at $8.30

Source & method
Susenas March 2026, weighted by persons; author's calculation. Expenditure per person is converted to national prices: expenditure × national poverty line ÷ the poverty line of the household's own province and urban or rural area. Lines per month: national Rp669,235; $4.20 = Rp786,301; $8.30 = Rp1,553,881 (2021 PPP, Rp6,155 per $). The World Bank adjusts prices by district, so its figures differ: for March 2025 this method gives 17.9% and 64.8% for Indonesia, against the World Bank's 15.5% and 64.1%.

Part 6 · Convergence

2015–2025: districts that started worse improved faster

Source & method
Susenas March 2015 and March 2025, author's calculation; official district poverty rates from BPS (485 districts with the same code in both years; 34 provinces on 2015 boundaries, province poverty computed from Susenas and equal to BPS). Mean expenditure per person is used as a proxy for income, because district income is not measured. Its 2015 value is divided by the province urban or rural poverty line. Real growth uses the national CPI (March 2025 / March 2015 = 1.343), so price differences between regions over time are not removed. Lines: least-squares fits for districts (solid) and provinces (dashed). A negative slope can also come from sampling error in the 2015 values.

Part 7 · Sector growth and poverty

Poverty fell more where manufacturing grew more, 2015–2025

Source & method
499 districts of Indonesia, one point each; no control variables. Vertical axis: annual change in the log of the official poverty rate × 100. Horizontal axis: the sector’s contribution to district GRDP growth (change in the sector’s value added 2015–2025, divided by total GRDP in 2015, per year). Slope from a regression on that sector alone. The shaded band is the 95% confidence interval of the fitted line, and the interval written in each panel is that of the slope; both use heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors. Correlations across districts: manufacturing −0.16, modern services 0.11, non-modern services 0.03, agriculture −0.02. The relation for single districts is weak: manufacturing growth explains about 2% of the differences in poverty change. Most districts have a manufacturing contribution below 1 point a year. Mining (slope −0.01) and construction (0.18) are not shown; neither is different from zero. Districts in the top and bottom 1% of total GRDP growth are dropped; a few points outside the axis ranges are not drawn. The 2-year periods are not shown as a raw scatter, because in the raw data all sectors fall together in 2019–21.

Part 7 · Sector growth and poverty

Manufacturing growth goes with falling poverty; other growth does not

Source & method
499 districts of Indonesia, 2015–2025; dependent variable: annual % change in the official poverty rate. Each sector is a separate regression of poverty change on growth from that sector, growth from all other sectors together, and the log of poverty in 2015. Upper bar: the sector. Lower grey bar: the rest of growth in the same regression. Total GRDP growth is from a regression with initial poverty only. Size: the typical difference in manufacturing growth between districts is 0.83 points a year, which goes with a 0.29% faster fall a year; the poverty change itself varies by 1.5% a year across districts. For construction, growth outside the sector is different from zero and construction growth is not. On the 475 districts that can also be matched to Susenas 2025, the manufacturing coefficient is −0.14 and not different from zero. Associations, not causal effects. Details are in the appendix.

Part 7 · Sector growth and poverty

$4.20 line: no source of growth has a clear relation with poverty

Source & method
475 districts that can be matched to Susenas in both years. Each sector is a separate regression of the annual % change in the poverty rate, 2015–2025, on growth from that sector, growth from all other sectors together, and the log of the poverty rate in 2015. Upper bar: the sector. Lower grey bar: the rest of growth in the same regression. Total GRDP growth is from a regression with initial poverty only. Standard errors robust to unequal variance. Poverty at this line is the author’s estimate from Susenas March 2015 and March 2025: expenditure per person at national prices (province urban and rural poverty lines as the price index), with the line moved from 2025 to 2015 by the national CPI. The mean district rate at this line fell from 46% in 2015 by 10% a year, and the fall varies by 6.3% a year across districts; in high-expenditure districts the rate is now a few percent, so percentage changes are large and imprecise. This is why the intervals are several times wider than at the other two lines. On the same 475 districts the manufacturing coefficient at the national line is −0.16 (0.10). Associations, not causal effects.

Part 8 · Falling formal employment in West Java

Formal work in West Java fell from 49.6% to 45.6% of workers

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015, 2019 and 2024, workers aged 15 and over; author's calculation. Formal: main-job status is employee, or employer with permanent workers. This is the status-based definition used in BPS labour releases; it does not measure contracts or social insurance. For Indonesia it gives 42.0% in 2024.

Part 8 · Falling formal employment in West Java

Manufacturing accounts for 3.2 of the 4.0-point fall

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, West Java, workers aged 15 and over; author's calculation. Each bar is the change in the sector's formal share weighted by its share of workers, plus the effect of its changing share of workers. The bars add up to the total change. Sector codes for 2019 have known errors and are not used. West Java added 5.6 million workers in the period; 1.8 million were formal.

Part 8 · Falling formal employment in West Java

The fall is among women, young workers and urban areas

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, West Java; author's calculation. Women were 31.5% of workers in 2015 and 37.3% in 2024. By employment status, the share of employees fell from 46.2% to 42.3%; own-account workers rose by 1.87 million and employees by 1.65 million.

Part 8 · Falling formal employment in West Java

Eight districts added 1.3 million workers and few formal workers

Source & method
Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, by district of residence; author's calculation. The eight districts had a formal share of 55% or more in 2015: Kota Depok, Kota Bogor, Kota Bekasi, Kab. Bekasi, Kota Bandung, Kota Cimahi, Kota Cirebon and Kab. Bogor. Formal manufacturing workers fell by 251,000 in these eight and rose by 387,000 in the other 19. District figures come from samples of a few hundred workers and have wide margins; the two group totals are more reliable.

Part 9 · Where manufacturing work went

Formal manufacturing workers: +637,000 in Central Java, +136,000 in West Java

Source & method
Workers: Sakernas August 2015 and 2024, by province of residence; author's calculation. Output: manufacturing value added at 2010 prices, BPS district GRDP summed to provinces. The two together suggest that labour-intensive manufacturing moved while capital-intensive manufacturing stayed; there are no district data by sub-industry to confirm this.

Appendix · Regression details

A7. How the regression is set up

10-year regression across districts
QuestionDoes poverty fall faster in districts where growth comes from a given sector?
Unit and period499 districts of Indonesia, one observation each; change from 2015 to 2025
Dependent variableAnnual % change in the official poverty rate: 100 × [ln(rate 2025) − ln(rate 2015)] ÷ 10
Explanatory variablesContribution of each sector to GRDP growth, points a year: (sector value added 2025 − 2015) ÷ total GRDP 2015 ÷ 10. Main slides: one sector and the rest of growth, which add up to total GRDP growth; one regression per sector. Appendix: seven sectors together
ControlLog of the poverty rate in 2015
EquationΔln Pi = α + βs gs,i + βr grest,i + γ ln Pi,2015 + εi
Reading βsChange in poverty, % a year, that goes with one more point of growth from sector s, with growth from the rest of the economy unchanged; βr is the same for one more point of growth from the rest
EstimationOrdinary least squares; standard errors robust to unequal variance; districts in the top and bottom 1% of GRDP growth dropped
Source & method
