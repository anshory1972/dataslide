3 · Checks

Above one half under each journal filter

Each percentage is the share of articles with exactly one author in , measured on a different set of journals or articles. Each column is a separate test. In every row, every column is above one half, so the result does not depend on the filter.

Subfield
The research area of each article, from its OpenAlex topic. Articles are classified, not journals: a philosophy article in a general humanities journal counts as philosophy.
All journals
All articles in that subfield in any journal, good or weak.
Top quarter
Only journals in the top 25% of their subfield, ranked by average citations per article over the past two years. This is the main quality filter, similar to “Q1”.
5+ references
Top-quarter journals, only articles with at least five references. Book reviews, editorials and short notes are often labelled “articles” and are mostly written by one person; this filter removes them.
Core
Only OpenAlex “core” journals, a list of established international journals from CWTS, Leiden University. It is independent of the citation ranking. It holds few humanities articles (Classics: 560 in five years), because it mainly covers international, English-language journals.
Articles (top quarter)
The number of articles in top-quarter journals in behind each share.

4 · Why

Three reasons, visible in the data

1. The work is done alone: no lab, no team, few partners

Research is reading, interpretation and writing. It needs no equipment and no division of labour. Few articles involve more than one institution.

Wuchty, Jones and Uzzi link the rise of teams in laboratory science to the rising cost and capital intensity of research, and report that single authors still wrote over 90% of arts and humanities papers (Science, 2007). In Canadian data, collaboration in the social sciences is closer to the natural sciences than to the humanities (Larivière, Gingras and Archambault 2006).

2. Little external funding, so no funded teams

Grants pay for research assistants, postdoctoral researchers and joint projects. In these subfields few articles report any funder.

Granting councils tend to copy programmes built for the natural sciences without taking into account how humanities research is done (Larivière, Gingras and Archambault 2006).

3. Careers are judged on single-authored books

Hiring and tenure in these fields rest on the monograph, usually written by one person. Books and book chapters are a large part of the output.

The Modern Language Association calls "solitary scholarship, the paradigm of one-author–one-work" deeply embedded in humanities practice, including tenure evaluation. In its survey, 88.9% of doctorate-granting departments rated a monograph important or very important for tenure (MLA 2007).

6 · Method

Data and definitions

  • Articles. OpenAlex works of type article in journals, 2000–2025: 28 million articles.
  • Share with one author. Articles with exactly one author divided by articles with at least one author.
  • Subfield. The primary topic of each article (252 OpenAlex subfields).
  • Top-quarter journals. Journals ranked by 2-year mean citations within their main subfield; the top 25% ( journals) form the tier. The 2026 ranking is used for all years.
  • Reasons data. Funders and institutions: articles in top-quarter journals published . Books: works of type book or book chapter, , any source.

7 · Replicate

Code and data

Download the package (zip, MB)

Files in the package
    1. Unzip, then install the packages (Python 3.10 or newer): 
      cd authorship-study
pip install -r requirements.txt
    2. Rebuild the tables and this page from the included counts (no API key needed): 
      cd analysis/authorship_oa
python 03_tables.py
python 05_report.py
    3. To pull the data again: get a free key at openalex.org, copy .env.example to .env and add the key, delete data/authorship_oa/processed, then run: 
      python 01_journals.py
python 02_counts.py
python 03_tables.py
python 04_reasons.py
python 05_report.py
      About 2,200 credits in total; a free key has 10,000 a day. Queries are cached, so a stopped run continues.
