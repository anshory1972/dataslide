Faster growth, higher human development, less poverty, less inequality, more formal jobs. Answer: Banten.

Source & method
Growth: BPS PDRB at constant 2010 prices (Seri 2010, linked to Seri 2000 before 2010); Indonesia is the sum of provinces. HDI: BPS var 2207 (life expectancy from the Census 2020 Long Form). Poverty: BPS var 192, March. Gini: BPS var 98, March, urban and rural. Informality: BPS var 2153, share of informal workers in all employment.

Part 1 · Everything looks good

Banten’s output grew faster than Indonesia’s

Output more than tripled in 25 years. Only Jakarta and Jawa Timur grew faster.

Source & method
BPS PDRB at constant 2010 prices, 2010–2025 (var 286); 2000–2009 from Seri 2000 (var 75) linked to Seri 2010 in 2010. Indonesia is the sum of provinces. 2012 boundaries.

Part 1 · Everything looks good

Poverty is low, and inequality is the lowest in Java

Banten’s poverty rate is two-thirds of the national rate. Its Gini ratio has fallen to 0.31.

Source & method
BPS var 192 (poverty rate, March) and var 98 (Gini ratio, March, urban and rural).

Part 2 · The number that does not fit

Then BPS asked people how happy they are

In 2021 Banten had the lowest happiness index of all 34 provinces: 68.08, against 71.49 for Indonesia.

Source & method
BPS, Indeks Kebahagiaan 2021 (var 601), from the Survei Pengukuran Tingkat Kebahagiaan (SPTK) 2021. The index combines life satisfaction, affect and meaning in life (eudaimonia), 0–100.

Part 3 · Reading the good numbers again

Less of Banten’s output reaches its households

Household consumption fell from 62% to 53% of PDRB, the largest fall in Java. More output went into factories, buildings and exports.

Source & method
BPS PDRB by expenditure, by province, current prices, 2010–2025, compiled in the indonesia-economic-data warehouse (CEIC export of BPS data). Over the same years Banten’s investment (gross fixed capital formation) rose from 30.0% to 34.4% of PDRB, and exports of goods and services (abroad and to other provinces) from 83% to 107%.

Part 3 · Reading the good numbers again

Output per person rose. Real wages fell.

Banten’s real wage fell 1.5% a year from 2016 to 2025, the largest fall of 33 provinces.

Source & method
Average monthly net wage of employees, Sakernas August, by province, deflated by each province’s household consumption deflator (current over constant 2010 household consumption in PDRB). indonesia-economic-data, analysis/real_wage_by_province.py. 2016 is the first year because of the 2015–2016 break in Sakernas wages. Banten’s nominal wage fell from Rp 4.39 million (August 2024) to Rp 4.06 million (August 2025), BPS var 2475; it was Rp 4.20 million in February 2026 (var 2819). Even in 2024, before that fall, the real wage was 3.5% below 2016 while real PDRB per capita was 23% above.

Part 3 · Reading the good numbers again

HDI’s own income component grew slowest in Java

Measured where people live, Banten’s spending per person grew slowest in Java. As a result, its HDI lead over Indonesia has almost gone: from 1.01 points in 2010 to 0.28 in 2024.

Source & method
BPS var 416: adjusted per capita expenditure, the income component of the Human Development Index, in constant 2012 prices and adjusted for price differences between regions. It is measured where people live (Susenas). Banten’s level still rises (Rp 10.78 million in 2010, Rp 13.50 million in 2025), but its lead over Indonesia fell from 14% to 5%. HDI: BPS var 494, new method, 2010–2024. Of the 0.73-point shortfall in Banten’s HDI gain against Indonesia’s over 2010–2024, the income component accounts for 0.81 points; health (+0.03) and education (+0.05) slightly offset it (log decomposition of the geometric mean).

Part 3 · Reading the good numbers again

In the cities, poverty and informal work have risen since 2019

Rural poverty kept falling. Urban poverty and urban informality are both above their 2019 levels.

Source & method
Poverty: BPS var 192, March. Informality: BPS Provinsi Banten var 975, August. Urban poverty rose from 4.12% (2019) to 5.21% (2026); urban informality from 33.8% (2019) to 40.8% (2025).

Part 4 · Why the people of Banten are unhappy

Not unhappy with what they have. Unhappy with how they live.

Income, job and education: rank 27–30. Worry, pressure, feeling happy, social relations, family harmony, control over life: rank 34.

Source & method
SPTK 2021 microdata, 74,684 respondents (Banten 1,652), survey weights. The 19 indicators of the BPS happiness index; worry and pressure are reversed so that higher is better. The index replicates the BPS provincial values to within 0.01. On worry, pressure and social relations the next-lowest province is DKI Jakarta.

Part 4 · Why the people of Banten are unhappy

Banten has formal jobs, but not good jobs

At the same pay, a better job means more happiness. Banten’s jobs are more formal than average, but less secure and less suited to the people doing them.

Source & method
SPTK 2021, workers only (53,383). Seven yes/no job features (R604a–g): skills match, matches interest, pay fair for the effort, pleasant workplace, safe, secure, easy to reach. In a regression with own earnings and household income classes, education, age, sex, marital status, household size, urban and province fixed effects, each additional feature adds 0.13 points of happiness; fair pay +0.27, pleasant workplace +0.24, interest +0.17, security +0.14. Being formal adds only +0.05.

Part 4 · Why the people of Banten are unhappy

The rich are fine. The lower-middle is left behind.

Households earning Rp 1.8–3 million a month, just above poverty, have the largest happiness gap.

Source & method
SPTK 2021, household income class (R609a), survey weights. Banten respondents per class: 198, 252, 380, 479, 343.

Part 4 · Why the people of Banten are unhappy

City life costs happiness, most of all in Banten and Kepri

At the same income, urban Bantenese are 0.43 points less happy than rural Bantenese, five times the national gap. More urban provinces are less happy.

Source & method
SPTK 2021. Left: coefficient on urban residence in survey-weighted regressions of happiness (0–10) on income class, education, age, sex, marital status and household size, estimated separately for each province (Indonesia: with province fixed effects). Right: province fixed effects from the national regression, against the urban share. Correlation −0.47 across 34 provinces; in a regression with manufacturing share, PDRB per capita and growth, the urban share stays significant (p = 0.006), manufacturing share has no effect, and growth is positive.

What to measure beyond output

What we foundWhat SE2026 and BPS could measure next
Output grows, but less reaches households; real wages fellWhere value added goes: wages, profits, owners outside Banten. Wages by place of residence and place of work
Formal jobs, but insecureContract type, outsourcing and job tenure by firm size and sector
The lower-middle is left behindIncome and job security of households just above the poverty line
City life costs happinessCommuting time, housing cost, public transport, and well-being by district

Growth is needed, but it is not enough. Measure how people live, not only what the economy produces.

Source & method
Sensus Ekonomi 2026 results are not yet available. The SPTK microdata has no district code; a district-level release would allow these questions to be answered within Banten.
