Implementation guide

Health and labour supply module for IndoTERM

Construction, implementation and simulation procedure

This guide specifies the procedure for introducing health status into the IndoTERM computable general equilibrium model of Indonesia, and for simulating the effect of energy transition policies on health, labour supply and output. The procedure comprises forty-three tasks in six stages. Each task states its procedure, the criterion by which its completion is verified, and, where applicable, its known failure modes.

The guide is written for direct execution. It assumes familiarity with general equilibrium modelling and with Indonesian household survey data. It does not assume prior knowledge of the labour-supply module specification, which is set out in section 5.

1Purpose and scope

The host model records the quantity of each fuel consumed by each industry, household group and region. It does not represent the effect of combustion on health, nor the effect of health on labour supply. The module specified in this guide supplies that representation.

The module classifies the working-age population by health status and labour-market status, and transfers the population between these classes from one year to the next. A policy that alters the incidence of illness therefore alters the composition of labour supply, and through labour supply it alters output, wages and employment by region.

Within scope. Construction of the module database from household survey and epidemiological sources; modification of the economic database; implementation of the module equations in the model code; execution and analysis of the baseline and policy simulations.

Outside scope. Estimation of the relation between emissions released in one province and ambient concentrations in another, and estimation of the relation between ambient concentration and disease incidence. These are inputs to the module. Their interface with the module is specified in task D.7; their estimation is not treated here.

5Theoretical basis of the module

Stage B constructs numerical arrays. Their construction is intelligible only once the specification they serve is understood. This section states that specification and provides three interactive figures in which the governing parameter may be varied and the response observed. The values in these figures are illustrative and are not estimates for Indonesia.

At the start of each year every person of working age occupies a category, defined by region, age, sex, health status and the activity undertaken in the preceding year. During the year each category makes offers: to remain in the same occupation, to move to another occupation, to be unemployed, or to withdraw from the labour force. Industry labour demand determines the number of positions available. Persons who held a position in the preceding year retain it in the first instance; the residual positions constitute vacancies and are allocated among the remaining offers. At the close of the year the transition matrix advances the population by one year of age, transfers a proportion between health states, and removes those who die. The result is the category distribution of the following year.

Health enters the specification at a single point, namely the transition matrix. All remaining effects follow from that entry.

5.1Allocation of labour offers

Each category allocates its members between destinations according to a constant elasticity of substitution share function. The share directed to a destination rises with the real after-tax wage available there and with a preference weight. Poor health reduces the preference weight attaching to every working destination simultaneously, so that the share withdrawing from the labour force rises.

Interactive figure

Figure 1Allocation of offers under the CES share function

Adjust the controls to observe the response of the offer distribution. The initial distribution reproduces the assumptions of the published specification: 88 per cent retain the same occupation, 7 per cent move occupation, and 3 per cent enter unemployment.

Interpretation. An increase in the relative wage of the alternative occupation redirects offers towards it, in proportion to the elasticity. A reduction in the capacity to work reduces the weight on all three working destinations simultaneously and raises the share withdrawing from the labour force. This gradient determines the magnitude of the policy result. It is estimated directly from the household survey in task B.5.

5.2Wage adjustment

The real wage does not clear the labour market within the year. It adjusts each year by a fraction α of the proportional gap between labour demand and labour supply, each measured relative to its baseline value. Until the adjustment is complete, an increase in labour supply is observed as unemployment rather than as employment.

Interactive figure

Figure 2Response to a permanent one per cent increase in labour supply

Labour supply rises permanently by one per cent in year one. The series show the deviation of employment and of the real wage from baseline in each subsequent year.

Employment Real wage

Interpretation. Deviation from baseline, per cent. The terminal values are determined by the elasticity of labour demand and are independent of α, which determines only the rate of convergence. The duration of the adjustment, rather than its terminal value, is the quantity of interest in an assessment of transition costs. Values of α between 0.5 and 0.6 are adopted in the published applications; both are examined in task E.8.

5.3Identification of transition rates without longitudinal data

The transition matrix requires two rates: the annual probability that a person in good health becomes ill, and the annual probability that an ill person recovers. No Indonesian survey observes the same individual in consecutive years, so neither rate is directly observed. The proportion of the population that is ill at each age is, however, the accumulated consequence of both rates operating over the life course. A sequence of annual cross-sections of that proportion therefore identifies the two rates jointly.

Interactive figure

Figure 3Recovery of the two rates from the age profile

The plotted points constitute a target age profile generated from a known pair of rates. Adjust the two controls until the simulated profile coincides with the target. Convergence demonstrates that both rates are identified from the age profile alone. This is the calibration performed in task B.8.

Simulated profile Target profile

Interpretation. Proportion of the population with a functional difficulty, per cent, by single year of age. At young ages a high incidence combined with a high recovery rate produces almost the same profile as a low incidence combined with a low recovery rate. The two rates are separated by the curvature of the profile at older ages. The full age range is therefore required, and the level is subsequently scaled to an external total as specified in task B.8.

Definition of a closure

The model contains more variables than equations. A closure is the set of variables determined outside the model. A different closure applied to the same model constitutes a different experiment. A swap exchanges the status of two variables: the first becomes endogenous and the second exogenous. The swaps listed above are the statements that convert a single-period model into a model solved recursively year by year.

7Stage B. Construction of the labour database

This stage converts three data sources into the arrays defined in Table 2. The method is that of the published South African application: the functional form of each rate is assumed, and its level is scaled to an external total of established reliability.

Input Susenas, Sakernas, GBD, BPS Output the module arrays in a single model data file Duration several months, one research assistant
Requirement applying to the whole stage

Every value that is not directly measured is to be entered in a register recording its value, its basis, and the external total to which it was scaled. The register is a deliverable of the project and is the condition under which the module can be assessed by a reviewer. It is compiled in task B.10 and is to be maintained throughout the stage.

TASK B.1

Define the module sets

ProcedureDetermine the five set definitions and record the justification for each. Subsequent revision of a set definition requires the reconstruction of every array.

CautionThe activity set must contain every occupation for which the economic model demands labour, and no others. Where the model specifies four occupations, the module supplies those four. The occupation set of the database is to be inspected before any survey processing is undertaken.

Treatment of informal employment

Approximately half of Indonesian employment is informal. The typical response of an informal worker to illness is a reduction in hours worked rather than cessation of employment. Were informal workers classified identically to formal workers, the effect of health on half of the workforce would not be represented. This constitutes the principal departure from the published specification.

TASK B.2

Specify the data request

ProcedureIssue three specific requests. A request that does not specify the blocks and the level of aggregation will be met with an unsuitable extract.

  • Susenas March core, 2018 to 2025, person level. Required blocks: functional difficulty, health complaint, and labour. The variables are to be harmonised across years, since names and block numbers are revised between rounds.
  • Susenas March core, 2013 to 2017. These are the final rounds recording the number of days of activity lost to illness. They are required once, for task B.9.
  • Sakernas, for occupation, earnings, the commencement date of current employment, and cessation of employment within the preceding year with the stated reason.

VerificationA March core file contains approximately 1.1 million person records. A file containing a few hundred thousand records is the September module, whose sample is approximately one quarter the size.

Revision of the questionnaire in 2018

Until 2017 the questionnaire recorded the number of days of activity lost to illness. From 2018 that item was discontinued and a block of eight graded functional-difficulty items was introduced, administered to all persons aged two years and above. The health status variable is therefore available from 2018 onward, whereas the hours-lost parameter must be derived from the 2013 to 2017 rounds and carried forward.

TASK B.3

Construct the activities table

ProcedureProduce a single weighted table of the population classified by region, age, sex, health status and activity. This table is the basis of the entire module database.

Two coding decisions are required.

  • Health status. The block contains eight domains, each with a graded response. Reduce these to three states by a stated rule and record the rule. The conventional rule assigns the most severe response across the eight domains.
  • Activity. From the labour block: employed, with occupation and formal or informal status; unemployed, short term or long term; outside the labour force; new entrant.
* Outline only. Variable names are revised between rounds;
* consult the codebook for the year being processed.

* 1. health status: most severe response across the eight domains
egen  worst = rowmax(diff1 diff2 diff3 diff4 diff5 diff6 diff7 diff8)
recode worst (1=1 "no difficulty") (2=2 "some difficulty") ///
             (3/4=3 "severe difficulty"), gen(h)

* 2. activity, from the labour block
gen     act = .
replace act = occ_group   if employed==1 & formal==1
replace act = occ_group+4 if employed==1 & formal==0
replace act = 9  if unemployed==1 & months_searching<=12
replace act = 10 if unemployed==1 & months_searching>12
replace act = 11 if new_entrant==1
replace act = 12 if not_in_labour_force==1

* 3. weighted table
collapse (sum) people=weight, by(region age sex h act)

VerificationThe table summed over health status and activity reproduces the published population by province for that year. Summed over health status alone it reproduces the published employment and unemployment totals. A discrepancy indicates an error in the weight variable or in the activity coding.

CautionCertain cells contain few observations, in particular young persons with severe difficulty in a specified occupation. These are to be identified at this point and pooled in task B.5, rather than permitted to determine a rate.

Comparison with the South African application

In the South African application health status and labour-market status were not jointly observed. National health prevalence was obtained from an external model and distributed across occupations by assumed proportions. The Susenas records both characteristics for the same individual, with region and age. The table constructed here therefore replaces the least defensible step of the published procedure.

TASK B.4

Derive lagged activities, categories and new entrants

ProcedureThree operations, in the order given.

  1. Lagged activities: divide the activities table by one plus the rate of growth of the labour force.
  2. Categories: premultiply lagged activities by the transition matrix. The transition matrix is not available until task B.8; this operation is therefore deferred and completed on return.
  3. New entrants: obtain the number of persons aged 15 by province from the BPS population projections. This is preferable to derivation as a residual.

VerificationCategories summed over all categories, plus new entrants, equals activities summed over all activities. A discrepancy indicates that the rows of the transition matrix do not sum correctly, which is diagnosed in task B.8.

TASK B.5

Construct the offer table and calibrate the health gradient

ProcedureThe offer table records intended flows, prior to the rationing of positions. It is constructed by rule rather than by estimation. Four rules are sufficient.

ProcedureThe final row determines the policy result. In the published application the offer strength by health status was assumed. Here it is estimated: compute the employment rate by health status, age, sex and region from the table constructed in task B.3, and set the offer strengths such that the module reproduces those employment rates.

VerificationEach row of the offer table sums to the size of the corresponding category. Offers represent intentions, and their column totals will not correspond to observed employment. That correspondence is established in task B.6.

TASK B.6

Derive realised flows by vacancy iteration

ProcedurePositions are limited, and the majority of offers are therefore not accepted. The number of vacancies and the number of persons retaining a position are mutually determined, and the system is solved by iteration to convergence.

  1. Assume an initial proportion of employees separated involuntarily, taken as three per cent unless the Sakernas data indicate otherwise.
  2. Compute the number of persons retaining the position held in the preceding year.
  3. Compute vacancies as industry labour demand less the number of persons retaining a position.
  4. Allocate vacancies among the remaining offers in proportion to those offers.
  5. Recompute retention given the movements now determined, and return to step 2.

VerificationTwo conditions. The iteration converges, and the column totals of the realised flow table reproduce the observed employment, unemployment and non-participation totals established in task B.3. Failure to converge indicates that the offer strengths determined in task B.5 are inconsistent with the number of positions available.

TASK B.7

Construct the ageing and mortality component of the transition matrix

ProcedureThis component is obtained by reference rather than by calibration. With ten-year age groups, approximately ten per cent of each group advances to the following group each year. Mortality rates are taken from the BPS life tables by age, sex and province, and verified against the Global Burden of Disease estimates.

VerificationApply the ageing and mortality component to the current population and compare the result with the BPS population projection for the following year. The two should correspond closely. A material discrepancy indicates that the age groups of the module and those of the life table are not aligned.

TASK B.8

Calibrate the health component of the transition matrix

This is the most demanding task in Stage B. Figure 3 presents the same problem in reduced form and should be worked through before commencement.

ProcedureFour steps.

  1. Functional form of incidence. Obtain incidence by cause, age and sex for Indonesia from the Global Burden of Disease study. Map the causes to the three functional-difficulty states. Respiratory and cardiovascular disease are of principal importance, being the causes responsive to ambient particulate concentration.
  2. Recovery. No source measures the recovery rate directly. It is treated as the parameter to be determined.
  3. Calibration. Select the recovery rate, and adjust incidence, such that the proportion of the population with a functional difficulty at each age implied by the module corresponds to the Susenas observation in each of the eight years from 2018 to 2025.
  4. Scaling of the level. Multiply both rates by a common factor such that the national number of cases by cause corresponds to the Global Burden of Disease total. The calibration determines the functional form; this step determines the level.

ProcedureThe rates may differ by activity. In the published application the progression rate was assumed to be lower for skilled workers. The gradient may instead be estimated: compute the variation of the proportion with a difficulty by labour-market status at each age, and impose it as a scaling, holding the national total constant.

VerificationFour conditions, each inexpensive to evaluate.

  • Each row of the matrix sums to one less the mortality rate. This is an arithmetic identity, not a matter of judgement.
  • The implied age profile corresponds to the Susenas observation in all eight years, and not solely in the year used for the calibration.
  • The implied prevalence corresponds to the Global Burden of Disease prevalence, which was not used in the calibration.
  • Advancing the module ten years without any shock leaves the distribution of health states stable. Drift indicates that incidence and recovery are mutually inconsistent.

CautionA single calibrated pair of rates is not to be reported as a measured quantity. The range of pairs reproducing the age profile to comparable accuracy is to be recorded. That range constitutes the sensitivity test specified in task E.8 and is the accurate statement of what the data determine.

TASK B.9

Construct the wage and effective labour tables

ProcedureTwo tables are required.

  • Wages. The Susenas does not record earnings. Relative wages between occupations are obtained from Sakernas; the absolute level is obtained from the wage bill recorded in the economic database. Relativities derive from the survey, the level from the national accounts.
  • Effective labour. One coefficient for each health status, expressing labour supplied per employed person relative to a person in good health. It is derived from the days of activity lost per episode of illness recorded in the 2013 to 2017 rounds, together with the number of days of inpatient care, which is recorded on every form.

VerificationEmployment by occupation multiplied by the wage reproduces the wage bill in the economic database, from which the level was obtained.

Function of the effective labour coefficient

In both published health applications, an improvement in health raised output solely by increasing the number of persons in employment. Productivity was unaffected. In an economy in which half of employment is informal, this omits the principal mechanism, since an ill informal worker generally remains employed and reduces hours. One coefficient per health status is sufficient to represent it.

TASK B.10

Compile the assumption register

ProcedureCompile a single table. Each value in the database that is not directly measured constitutes one entry, recording the object, the value adopted, the basis of that value, and the external total to which it was scaled. The register of the published application contains eighteen entries; the present register will contain fewer, several quantities assumed there being measured by the Susenas.

VerificationThe register is complete when an independent reader can trace every unmeasured value in the database to a stated basis.

TASK B.11

Write the module arrays to a model data file

ProcedureThe model reads binary data files. Write each array to a separately identified header within a single data file: activities, lagged activities, categories, offers, realised flows, the transition matrix, and wages.

VerificationOpen the file with the data inspection utility supplied with the modelling software and confirm the dimensions of each array against the set definitions recorded in task B.1. A transposed dimension produces a model that compiles, solves, and is incorrect.

CautionThe module is computationally small: approximately eight regions, fifteen activities, five age groups, two sexes and three health states yield approximately 3,600 category cells. Computational capacity is not a constraint in this project at any point.

8Stage C. Modification of the economic database

The existing database was aggregated for the analysis of electricity generation. It aggregates away the ore sectors, the metal sectors, the health sectors and the extraction provinces, all of which are required here. Two mapping files are revised and the database pipeline is re-executed.

Input two mapping files Output a reconstructed and verified database Duration several weeks
Governing principle

The model database is not to be edited directly under any circumstance. The input files are edited and the pipeline is re-executed. The pipeline reconstructs trade flows, transport margins and balances, and the verification program then confirms every accounting identity. Direct editing transfers responsibility for every identity to the modeller, with no mechanism by which a violation would be detected.

TASK C.1

Define the revised sector mapping

VerificationThe mapping assigns each of the 185 sectors to exactly one aggregated sector. A sector assigned to none, or to more than one, is the most frequent error and terminates the pipeline.

  • East and South Kalimantan as a single separate region, being the location of coal extraction.
  • Central and Southeast Sulawesi as a single separate region, being the location of nickel extraction.
  • Papua separately identified. Sumatra, the remainder of Kalimantan, the remainder of Sulawesi, and the eastern islands as previously defined.

TASK C.4

ProcedureExecute the verification program supplied with the pipeline. It evaluates every accounting identity in the database: that supply equals deliveries, that transport margins attach to existing trade flows, and that every balance closes.

VerificationZero failures. No tolerance is applied. A failure at this point constitutes an error in the database which is transmitted to every simulation subsequently executed.

Diagnostic value of the consistency checks

In an earlier application of this pipeline, the verification program detected a modification that had been introduced into the quantity equation but not into the corresponding price equation. The simulation had solved without reporting an error. The verification program was the only mechanism that identified the divergence of the accounts.

TASK C.5

CautionThis procedure yields emissions by region of origin, which is not equivalent to exposure by region of residence. The relation between the two is the source–receptor allocation identified in Table 3 as unassigned. The equivalence of emissions and exposure is not to be adopted by default; the allocation of that task is to be resolved by the project team.

TASK D.3

Implement the category transition equation

ProcedureThe first of the three governing equations. Categories in the current year equal activities in the preceding year premultiplied by the transition matrix. The activity classification is retained; age and health status are advanced, and a proportion of the population is removed by mortality.

! Current categories = preceding activities advanced by the transition matrix.
! d_transit is the change in a transition rate. It is the sole
! point at which a health policy enters the module.
Equation E_cat
  (all,o,ACTV)(all,a,AGE)(all,g,SEX)(all,d,DST)(all,h,HLT)
  CAT(o,a,g,d,h) * cat(o,a,g,d,h)
    = sum{aa,AGE, sum{hh,HLT,
        ACTL(o,aa,g,d,hh) * TRAN(o,aa,g,d,hh,a,h)
          * [actl(o,aa,g,d,hh) + d_transit(o,aa,g,d,hh,a,h)] }};

VerificationSolve the model with no shock applied. Every result must equal zero. A non-zero result indicates that the rows of the transition matrix do not sum to one less the mortality rate, which is corrected in task B.8.

TASK D.4

Implement the offer equations

ProcedureThe second governing equation, presented in reduced form as Figure 1. Each category allocates its members between destinations according to the wage available and a preference weight.

Two restrictions apply. Persons outside the labour force offer only to remain outside it, subject to the return flow specified below. Offers are restricted to occupations of comparable skill, so that a labourer does not offer to a managerial occupation.

Departure from the published specification

In the South African module no return flow from outside the labour force is permitted. In Indonesia the majority of persons outside the labour force are women engaged in domestic work and students, for whom re-entry is common. A return flow is therefore permitted, with a low offer weight. That weight is to be recorded in the register compiled in task B.10.

VerificationApply a one per cent increase to the wage in a single occupation, with no other shock. Offers to that occupation increase, offers to the remaining destinations decrease, and the total across destinations is unchanged. That total is the size of the category, which this equation cannot alter.

TASK D.5

Implement vacancy determination and rationing

ProcedureVacancies equal industry labour demand less the number of persons retaining the position held in the preceding year. Flows of persons not previously holding the position are allocated in proportion to the offers directed to that occupation. Unsuccessful applicants enter unemployment; unsuccessful applicants already unemployed enter long-term unemployment.

VerificationFlows into each occupation, summed, equal industry labour demand for that occupation. This equation block connects the module to the remainder of the model, and an inequality here produces a model that solves while the accounts diverge.

TASK D.6

Connect the module to the regional labour market

ProcedureTwo substitutions, and no others.

  1. The regional labour market equation presently determines labour supply from a wage elasticity. Its supply side is replaced by the sum of accepted offers obtained from the module.
  2. The national wage adjustment rule is replaced by the rule presented as Figure 2, specified by occupation and by region rather than nationally.

TASK D.7

Implement the baseline and policy health interfaces

ProcedureThe module requires two distinct interfaces for the receipt of health information.

  • Baseline interface. The model is required to follow a trajectory of illness determined externally. Introduce a shift variable on the incidence rate, render it endogenous, and shock the number of new cases to the required trajectory. Offset the shift within the rate of remaining in good health, so that the rows of the matrix continue to sum correctly.
  • Policy interface. The model is required to receive a deviation from that trajectory, obtained from the pollution calculation. The same shift variable is rendered exogenous and is shocked directly with the change in incidence by age, sex and region.

VerificationApply a deviation of zero in a policy simulation. Every result must equal zero and the simulation must reproduce the baseline exactly. A non-zero result indicates an error in the offsetting of the rate of remaining in good health.

Function of the interface in the overall design

Every element upstream of the module, comprising fuel consumption, emissions, ambient concentrations and the exposure–response functions, resolves to a single quantity: the change in incidence by age, sex and region. The module receives that quantity and requires no further information. Its operation is identical whether the change in health status originates in ambient air pollution, in mining activity, or in the allocation of revenue to health services. This property permits three dissimilar transmission channels to share a single mechanism.

TASK D.8

Implement the effective labour and health service extensions

ProcedureTwo extensions, neither present in the published applications.

  • Effective labour. Multiply each employed person by the effective labour coefficient for that health status obtained in task B.9, so that an improvement in health raises labour supplied per person as well as the number of persons employed.
  • Health service demand. Specify demand for the private and public health sectors as a function of the number of persons in poor health, at a stated cost per case, so that illness avoided is also expenditure avoided.

CautionA shift variable is to be introduced into the price equation and the quantity equation together. A shift variable introduced into the quantity equation alone produces a model that solves and subsequently fails the database consistency check, quantities having adjusted without a corresponding price signal and having diverged from expenditure.

TASK D.9

Compile, compare and submit for review

ProcedureCompile until no errors are reported. Execute the file comparison introduced in task D.1 and examine every difference. Submit the module equations for review by a specialist in this class of model. The project provides for such a review.

VerificationThree conditions before Stage D is regarded as complete: the model compiles without error; the comparison reports only intended modifications; and a simulation with no shock applied returns zero for every result.

10Stage E. Execution of the simulations

Every reported result is the difference between two solutions of the same model, one without the policy and one with it. The baseline is therefore not a preliminary exercise; it constitutes one half of every reported quantity.

Input the completed model and database Output one baseline, four policy simulations, sensitivity analysis Duration several weeks of machine time

TASK E.1

Revise the closure for module-determined labour supply

ProcedureRemove the swap statement recorded in task A.5, by which national labour supply was determined by the real wage. That determination is now performed by the module, by occupation and by region. The remainder of the closure is unchanged.

VerificationThe number of exogenous variables equals the number of equations. The validation procedure evaluates this condition in seconds.

TASK E.2

Validate all closures and shock files

ProcedureExecute the closure and shock validation procedure against the baseline closure, against each policy closure, and against each shock file. The procedure identifies any variable that is shocked without being exogenous, and any discrepancy in the count of exogenous variables.

VerificationAll files pass. A simulation that terminates in its fourth year on a closure error consumes a full day of machine time. This validation requires approximately one minute.

TASK E.3

Execute the baseline simulation

ProcedureSolve the model year by year from 2023 to 2050 under the existing baseline shocks, together with the imposed trajectory of incidence specified in task D.7.

VerificationFour conditions, in the order given.

  • The macroeconomic aggregates follow the trajectories obtained in task A.4. The introduction of the module must not alter the baseline economy.
  • The distribution of the population across health states follows the imposed trajectory.
  • Employment by occupation and region grows at a plausible rate, with no discontinuity in any year.
  • The database consistency checks pass in every year, not solely in the first.

CautionWhere the macroeconomic baseline is altered by the introduction of the module, the module is exerting an influence in the baseline that it should not exert. This is to be diagnosed before any policy simulation is executed. A contaminated baseline renders every policy result incorrect by an unknown and constant amount.

TASK E.4

Execute policy simulation 1: removal of the fossil fuel subsidy

ProcedureA fuel subsidy is a negative commodity tax on fuel purchases. Its removal is a change in that tax, specified by fuel and by user. This is the instrument already employed for the carbon price, and no additional model structure is required.

The health consequence is then applied: reduced combustion yields reduced emissions, reduced emissions yield reduced ambient concentrations, reduced concentrations yield reduced incidence, and the change in incidence is the shock applied to the interface implemented in task D.7.

VerificationExecute the simulation twice, once with the health interface shocked to zero and once with the health effect applied. The difference between the two solutions is the health benefit, separated from the direct effect of the increase in fuel prices.

TASK E.5

Execute policy simulation 2: expansion of mining and smelting

ProcedureShock the output of the ore and metal sectors separately identified in task C.1. The health consequence operates in the opposite direction to simulation 1: increased particulate and water contamination in the extraction regions yields increased incidence, concentrated in a small number of regions.

VerificationThe regional distribution is the diagnostic. The loss of health should be confined to the extraction regions, and the increase in employment should arise in the same regions. A health effect distributed uniformly across the country indicates that the exposure calculation is employing national averages where regional values are required.

TASK E.6

Execute policy simulation 3: allocation of revenue to health

ProcedureBoth policies generate revenue, comprising the subsidy forgone and the tax on mineral extraction. Allocate that revenue to health expenditure by region, and convert it into a change in incidence or recovery using the elasticity supplied by Work Package 2.2.

CautionThe elasticity is to be requested in the form required by the module, namely the effect on annual incidence or on recovery per unit of health expenditure per capita, by region. An elasticity expressed in any other form requires conversion, and the conversion is the point at which errors are introduced.

VerificationThe revenue expended in this simulation equals the revenue accruing to the government accounts of the model. Both are to be read from the results and compared. Double counting of the revenue is a frequent error and is difficult for a reader to detect.

TASK E.7

Execute the control simulations without the health channel

ProcedureExecute each policy simulation a second time with the health interface disabled. These simulations are required, not optional. They constitute the sole means by which the contribution of the health channel can be quantified, that contribution being the subject of the project.

VerificationThe control simulation reproduces the result that a conventional energy study would obtain. A substantial divergence indicates that the module is influencing the result through an unintended channel.

TASK E.8

Execute the sensitivity analysis

ProcedureFour tests, each corresponding to a quantity that is assumed rather than measured.

VerificationEvery reported quantity is accompanied by a range. A result preserved across all four tests constitutes a finding. A result obtained only at a single set of assumed values constitutes a description of those values.

11Stage F. Analysis and reporting

A dynamic simulation generates every variable for every year and every region. The greater part of that output does not constitute a result. These four tasks reduce the output to a reportable form.

Input the solved simulations Output tables, figures and the assumption register Duration several weeks

TASK F.1

Extract deviations from baseline

ProcedureFor each policy simulation, report the deviation from baseline by year, for output, employment by occupation and region, wages, the population in each health state, and mortality.

CautionWhere the baseline and the policy are presented as two trajectories on a single figure, they are to be drawn as two paths diverging from a common origin. A vertical discontinuity at the year of the policy is not to be used, and the year of the policy is not to be given a separate axis division.

TASK F.2

Decompose the health and direct effects

ProcedureUsing the control simulations executed in task E.7, decompose each reported quantity into three components: the direct effect of the energy or mining policy, the additional effect of the change in health status, and the interaction of the two.

VerificationThe three components sum to the total. A failure to sum indicates that a channel is active in a simulation in which it was intended to be disabled.

TASK F.3

Publish the assumption register with the results

ProcedureInclude the register compiled in task B.10 in the published output. Each entry states the value adopted, its basis, the external total to which it was scaled, and the sensitivity range established in task E.8.

Function of the register in the published output

The documentation of the South African application continues to be cited fifteen years after publication. This is attributable not to any unusual property of the model but to the presence of every assumed value in a table, each accompanied by a statement of its origin. A reader who disputes a particular value can identify precisely which result is affected. That property is what permits a module to be used by parties other than its authors.

TASK F.4

Assemble the reproduction package

ProcedureAssemble a single directory containing the model source and the reference source from which it was derived, the two mapping files, the module data file, every closure and shock file, the command files for each simulation, and an instruction file stating the order of execution.

VerificationProvide the directory to a colleague not involved in its construction and request the reproduction of one reported quantity. Any question that colleague is obliged to ask identifies an omission from the instruction file.

AAppendix A. Glossary

Each term is defined at its first occurrence in the text. The definitions are collected here for reference.

Activity
The labour-market state occupied during the year: an occupation, unemployment, or non-participation.
Baseline
A solution of the model in the absence of the policy. Every reported result is the difference between a policy solution and the baseline.
Calibration
The selection of an unobserved parameter such that the model reproduces an observed quantity.
Category
A group of persons defined by region, age, sex, health status and the activity undertaken in the preceding year.
Closure
The set of variables determined outside the model. The same model under a different closure constitutes a different experiment.
Effective labour
Labour supplied per employed person, expressed relative to a person in good health.
Elasticity
The proportional response of one quantity to a proportional change in another.
Emission factor
The quantity of a pollutant released per unit of fuel consumed.
Exposure–response function
The relation between the ambient concentration to which a population is exposed and its rate of disease.
Functional difficulty
The Susenas block of eight graded items covering sight, hearing, mobility, manual dexterity, cognition, behaviour, speech and self-care. It defines the health status variable used here.
Incidence
The annual probability that a person in good health enters a state of illness.
Incumbent
A person retaining the position held in the preceding year.
Recovery rate
The annual probability that a person in a state of illness returns to good health. Not observed in Indonesia and therefore calibrated.
Shift variable
A variable introduced into an equation so that a shock may be applied to it. It is to be introduced into the price and quantity equations together.
Source–receptor matrix
The allocation of a unit of emissions released in one province to the ambient concentration of each province.
Sticky wage
A wage that adjusts gradually, such that for a period the level of employment is determined by demand rather than by the wage.
Swap
The exchange of the exogenous or endogenous status of two variables within a closure.
Transition matrix
The matrix of probabilities that a person of given age and health status occupies each health status one year later, or has died.
Vacancy
A position not filled by the person who held it in the preceding year.
