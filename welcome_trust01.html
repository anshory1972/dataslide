Work package 2.3

Modelling health and labour in Indonesia's energy transition

SDGs Center, Universitas Padjadjaran · CoPS, Victoria University

Financing a Just Green Transition. This work package measures the effects of fossil fuel subsidy reform and critical mineral taxation on the Indonesian economy. The model is extended to represent health status and labour supply.

Work package 2.3

Objective

Quantify the impact of alternative ways of raising and allocating revenue on health, equity, the environment and climate, using a computable general equilibrium model.

What the proposal states

WP2.2 estimates the causal effect of past reforms. WP2.3 simulates future reform scenarios: mining expansion, critical mineral taxation, and fossil fuel subsidy removal. The scenarios are co-developed with stakeholders in WP1.5.

Five steps

1 Update and expand the Indonesia CGE model.
2 Translate the scenarios into model inputs.
3 Simulate each scenario.
4 Review assumptions with stakeholders.
5 Test sensitivity of the results.

Link 1 is a dose–response relation. It comes from the other work packages. WP2.3 builds links 2 and 3, and cannot start link 2 until link 1 is delivered in a form the model can read.

What the model reports

Economy: GDP, employment. Environment: emissions, pollution. Health: mortality, morbidity. Each one by region, by household group, and year by year.

Are the scenarios fixed?

The proposal states that the scenarios come from WP1.5. Can WP2.3 adjust them, or add scenarios, once the model is built?

The work packages have to be aligned

Link 1 is the interface. WP2.1, WP2.2 and WP2.3 must agree on the same policy definitions, the same health measure, and the same population groups. Without that agreement the chain does not connect.

What WP2.3 contributes

Link 2: health status to labour supply. Link 3: labour supply to economy-wide outcomes. This is what a CGE model is suited to.

The model

IndoTERM

A bottom-up computable general equilibrium model of the Indonesian economy.

Model

A system of non-linear equations. N equations, N + X variables. N are endogenous, X are exogenous. Typically hundreds of thousands of equations.

Equilibrium

Demand meets supply, and prices clear. XDir(Pir) = XSir(Pir)

34 provinces. Each one carries its own markets and its own prices.

General

Not partial. Every sector's market is linked through the input-output structure.

Bottom-up

Each region clears its own markets, so prices differ between regions. This is why the model is large. The E in TERM stands for Enormous.

Economy-wide

National aggregates such as GDP can be reported.

Computable

Too large to solve by hand. A computer solves it.

The model

What IndoTERM already solves, and where health can enter

Every block below is already built. Health can enter through one of them.

Households supply labour and buy goods. Industries combine intermediate inputs, energy, capital and land. Regions trade with each other and with the world, and the model is solved one year at a time. Labour supply is where health can enter, by grouping people by age, health status and work status. Whether we get there depends on the data.

Literature review

How health enters a CGE model

Health does not sit outside the economy. Four channels carry it back in. The literature covers some of them and not others.

Each step needs its own data. The last step is where the economic model does its work.

Morbidity: working time lost

Sick workers lose days. Family members lose days caring for them. Both enter as assumed days per case.

Dixon 2010 · Verikios 2010

Mortality: a smaller labour force

Deaths remove workers from every later year. This is the long-run channel.

Verikios 2013 · Roos & Giesecke 2014

Productivity at work

A worker who is present but unwell produces less. Count each episode once: as time lost or as lower productivity, never both.

Resosudarmo 2003

Medical spending

Households and government pay for treatment. That shifts demand and public budgets.

Li 2003 · Xie 2016

Found in the literature

  • Cause-specific mortality
  • Morbidity as working time lost
  • Hospitalisations rate × unit cost
  • Medical spending

Not found in the literature

  • Road traffic mortality and morbidity
  • Infant mortality

Deliver what is proven. Do not promise what no study has built.

Literature review

Five options, and the route we take

The options can be combined. Each one needs different data and a different amount of work.

A. Satellite health accounting

Health outcomes reported after each run, with no link back to the economy. Low effort, and it gives a benchmark to check the rest against.

Tian 2018 · Xie 2019

B. Exposure–health feedback

Morbidity to working time. Medical costs to demand. Mortality to next year's labour. By province and by year.

Li 2003 · Xie 2016 · Qu 2020

C. Health-state labour module

People are grouped by age, health status and work status. Transitions between groups each year drive labour supply. This carries long-run mortality. It needs health transitions over time.

Verikios 2013 · Roos & Giesecke 2014

D. Productive health spending

Health spending raises effective labour. This matters for scenarios that allocate revenue to health.

Rutten & Reed 2009 · Kabajulizi 2016

E. Household microsimulation

Prices and wages from the model feed a household model, for poverty and inequality.

Yusuf 2008 · Ahmed 2018

What IndoTERM already has

Labour-supply shifters, regional population, and wage adjustment. It has no health block, and no labour module by age and health state.

The route we take: health status to labour supply

Option C, fed by option B. Pollution changes health status. Health status changes participation and hours. The model turns that into economy-wide and regional results. Option A runs alongside as a check.

  • Exposure–response functions
  • Health transitions over time the binding constraint
  • Participation and hours by age
