IndoTERM technical note · August 2026

Simulating the BSK Import Gateways: Method and Pitfalls

Arief Anshory Yusuf

Universitas Padjadjaran

The government wants imports of selected goods to enter through Bitung, Sorong and Kupang. Making that policy simulable in a regional CGE model turns out to be the hard part: the obvious shock is the wrong one, the data says the gateways barely exist, and the model must be extended before the question can be asked at all. This note documents each step.

Context

The policy, and what this note is for

In November 2024 the Minister of Industry announced a plan to move the entry points for several import categories — electronics, textiles and garments, footwear, cosmetics, ceramics and leather goods — from Java to three eastern ports: Sorong, Bitung and Kupang (Kompas, BeritaSatu). This note is a methods companion: it documents how the policy was made simulable in IndoTERM (a TERM-family interregional CGE model of Indonesia, here aggregated to 23 commodities and 7 regions, with the three port provinces kept separate), the modelling traps met on the way, and how large a reallocation the data can support. Policy results belong in a separate note; this one is about method.

Specification

It is an xtrad shock, not a ximps shock

TERM gives every import flow two regional coordinates: the region where it lands and the region where it is used. The policy is about the first; the tempting shock is on the second. Choosing wrongly changes the experiment entirely.

TERM's trade matrix TRADE(c,s,r,d) records commodity c from source s (dom or imp) moving from region r to region d. For imports, r is the port of entry. The base data show why this dimension is the policy's home: 74 percent of electronics imports land in Jakarta and 19 percent in Kepulauan Riau, and 78 percent of all electronics imports are used in a different province from the one where they land. Landing and use are different economic events.

The variable ximps(c,d) is imports used in d. Shocking it "reallocates" imports by forcing the port provinces to consume them. In a trial run of that specification, holding national imports fixed while cutting other regions 10 percent forced the three ports' import use up several hundred percent, required tariff-equivalent wedges of −55 to −70 percent, and made the port provinces the largest losers in the country (real GDP −1.4 percent) as subsidised imports crushed their local production. The model eventually refused outright: domestic output (MAKE) went negative. None of that is the announced policy.

The correct object is xtrad(c,"imp",r,d) — or in aggregate, landings by port xtrad_d(c,"imp",r). A first check with the route-cost variable atrad confirmed the contrast: making BSK routes 5 percent cheaper moved landings on existing routes by +18 to +23 percent with no measurable national effect at all (GDP 0.0000, CPI −0.0001). Rerouting landings is a logistics experiment; forcing consumption is a demolition.

Data

Zero import landings, and where to fix them

A percentage-change model cannot grow a cell whose base value is zero: the cell has zero weight in every linearised equation, so no price, subsidy or preference shock can create a flow that is not in the data. The base year says most BSK gateway routes do not exist.

Base-year import landings, Rp billion. TERM enforces the zeros structurally: TRADMAR(c,s,m,r,d)=0 wherever TRADE(c,s,r,d)=0, so a route without a flow has no margins either.

Where to modify the database matters as much as whether. Editing the aggregated trade matrix TRAD directly means maintaining every accounting identity by hand: import totals by region of use, delivered values, margin demands on each route, margin supply by producing region. It can be done — it was done, as a first attempt — but every rule is the modeller's responsibility. The better interception point is upstream, in regsupp.har: its header MSHR holds IMPSHR(c,r), the share of national imports of product c entering through port r, built from customs data. The pipeline program reg1 reads these shares, renormalises them, and the trade-matrix construction (gravity allocation plus RAS balancing) then builds routes, destination patterns and margins itself; the standard checks (CHKMDA/B) certify every identity afterwards. Change the shares, rerun the pipeline, and consistency is the machinery's job, not yours.

The seeding tool is a 60-line TABLO program, regsupmod.tab, driven by rnregsupmod.bat. It reads the original shares, the national import value of each product from national.har (the imp slices of 1BAS/2BAS/3BAS/5BAS/6BAS), and the product-to-sector mapping from the aggregation file, then applies one rule:

! floor: each port lands at least Rp 1 billion of each product
Formula
 (all,c,POLICY)(all,r,PORTS: V0IMP(c)>1 and MSHR(c,r)*V0IMP(c)<1)
   MSHR(c,r) = 1/V0IMP(c);
! then renormalise every row of shares to sum to one
 (all,c,COM) TOTS(c) = sum{r,ORG, MSHR(c,r)};
 (all,c,COM: TOTS(c)>0)(all,r,ORG) MSHR(c,r) = MSHR(c,r)/TOTS(c);

A floor, not an addition: cells already above Rp 1 billion (ceramics and electronics at Bitung and Sorong) are left untouched. The renormalisation spreads the offset across all existing ports in proportion, so no donor port has to be chosen. After rerunning the pipeline, every seeded route exists with roughly Rp 1 billion of flow, margins and all — and both consistency checks pass with zero failures.

Model

Modifying TERM.TAB: a shifter that can hold landings

To force landings to a chosen path, the landings variable must be swappable against an instrument. In the standard TAB it is not: the model must be extended, and the extension must respect price–quantity consistency.

Two obstacles. First, xtrad is condensed away (Substitute xtrad using E_xtrad) — a substituted variable cannot be swapped or shocked. Second, the aggregate xtrad_d is backsolved (Backsolve xtrad_d using E_xtrad_d) — backsolved variables are not in the condensed system either. Two changes fix this. The Backsolve statement is disabled, keeping xtrad_d in the system (23×2×7 = 322 extra variables; negligible). And a new shifter fxtrad_d(c,s,r) — one value per commodity, source and origin, uniform over destinations — is added wherever atrad appears, entering price and quantity identically:

! E_puse: the user-price (CES) index over origins
ID01(DELIVRD_R(c,s,d))*puse(c,s,d)
  = sum{r,ORG, DELIVRD(c,s,r,d)*[pdelivrd(c,s,r,d)+atrad(c,s,r,d)+fxtrad_d(c,s,r)]};

! E_xtrad: route demand
xtrad(c,s,r,d) - atrad(c,s,r,d) - fxtrad_d(c,s,r)
  = xuse(c,s,d) + srctwist(c,s,r,d) - avesrctwist(c,s,d)
  - SIGMADOMDOM(c)*[pdelivrd(c,s,r,d)+atrad(c,s,r,d)+fxtrad_d(c,s,r)-puse(c,s,d)];

The price term is not optional. A first version added the shifter to the quantity equation only. It solved — and then failed the use-equals-deliveries check (RATB) on the first data update: quantities were being rationed with no price signal, so purchases and deliveries drifted apart. Entering exactly like atrad makes the shifter a route cost, which keeps the accounts closed. It also fixes its interpretation: an iceberg cost that burns real resources. GDP losses under this instrument are therefore an upper bound; a rent-generating instrument (a route tax) would show smaller losses.

The closure side is then three statements in the command file — and one rule learned the hard way: swap only cells whose base landings are positive, or the matrix is singular.

Exogenous fxtrad_d;
swap fxtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT) = xtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT);
shock xtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT) = uniform -15;
! hold each product's national import volume
swap tuser_ud(POLICY,"imp") = natximp(POLICY);

Caution

Why RunDynam is not recommended here (yet)

The temptation is to take the modified model straight into a year-on-year dynamic run. With this database, resist it: do the comparative statics properly first.

  • The statics already run at the edge. Even one-period solves need Gragg's method with five subintervals, and accuracy still sits at 6 of 10. The cause is structural: seeded cells worth Rp 1 billion absorb reallocations worth hundreds of billions, so their percentage changes are enormous, and enormous percentage changes are exactly what a linearised solver handles worst.
  • Dynamics compounds the problem. In a RunDynam sequence those extreme changes recur period after period on an updating database, errors accumulate across years, and a single assertion failure (MAKE>=0, RATA/RATB) aborts the whole baserun or policy run midway — failures of exactly the kind met repeatedly in the static work.
  • The closure is nonstandard. The fxtrad_d/xtrad_d and tuser_ud/natximp swaps must be carried into RunDynam's closure files for every period; mistakes are silent until a run dies hours in.
  • If dynamics is pursued anyway: aggregating the commodities is a must — fewer, larger cells shrink the multipliers that cause the trouble. Beyond that: seed larger initial flows (defend a bigger assumed gateway capacity), split the shock across periods so no single year forces an extreme change, and verify the accounting checks after every period, not only at the end.

Strategy

Finding the largest feasible shock

With Rp 1 billion starting cells, large reallocations are infeasible by construction: the displaced volume must squeeze through gateways of negligible capacity, and at some point the receiving economies cannot absorb and re-ship it. The model says so through its accounting assertions. The strategy is therefore a search: the largest cut that still solves, one product at a time, and then all together.

Joint cut (six products)Outcome
−5%solves, residual 2.4e−05
−10%solves, residual 3.1e−05
−15%solves, residual 1.5e−05
−20%, −22%fail: RATB (use ≠ deliveries)
−25%fail: RATA (Footwear × Sorong output)

The failure modes are economics, not numerics: RATA/RATB are the accounting identities that break when a region is asked to handle more volume than its output and margin capacity allows. Electronics is the extreme case — its imports are Rp 159 trillion nationally against Rp 3 billion of BSK landings, so even a 5 percent joint cut fails when electronics is included; it is searched separately below.

Bisection between 1 and 95 percent; a cut is feasible when the solve completes AND the maximum residual ratio is below 1e-3. The ceiling scales with the ratio of existing BSK landings to national imports: ceramics starts with Rp 47 billion at the ports, electronics with Rp 3 billion against Rp 159 trillion of imports. An earlier, looser criterion (completion only) accepted a Cosmetics run whose residual was 1.0 — completion is not convergence. Joint run residual at −15: 1.48e-05.

Implications

What this method settles

  • Specification first. The gateway policy lives on the landing dimension of the trade matrix. A use-side shock produces a different, harsher and wrong experiment. This distinction is invisible in national models; it is the reason to use a TERM-class model at all.
  • The data is the binding constraint. Zero base landings mean the policy cannot be simulated without a seeding assumption, and every result inherits that assumption. Rp 1 billion per port is deliberately minimal; the feasible-shock ceilings above are its direct consequence. A larger assumed starting capacity raises every ceiling.
  • Seed upstream. Editing the port-share matrix and letting the data pipeline rebuild routes, margins and balances is safer than editing trade matrices by hand — the machinery enforces what the modeller would otherwise have to remember.
  • Read assertion failures as findings. MAKE<0 and RATA/RATB failures marked exactly where scenarios stopped being economically possible. They are the model refusing an impossible question, and the refusal is informative.
