Simulating the BSK Import Gateways: Method and Pitfalls

Arief Anshory Yusuf · Universitas Padjadjaran

The government wants imports of selected goods to enter through Bitung, Sorong and Kupang. Making that policy simulable in a regional CGE model is the hard part: the obvious shock is the wrong one, the data says the gateways barely exist, and the model must be extended before the question can be asked. Browse with PageDown; each screen is one step of the argument.

Context

November 2024: move the import gateways east

The Minister of Industry announced that entry points for electronics, textiles and garments, footwear, cosmetics, ceramics and leather goods would move from Java to Sorong, Bitung and Kupang. This deck documents the method: how the policy was made simulable in IndoTERM (23 commodities × 7 regions, the three port provinces kept separate), the traps met on the way, and how large a reallocation the data can support. Results belong in a separate note; this is about method.

Three things make this policy unusually demanding to model. It is spatial — a national model cannot even state the question. It concerns a dimension of the data (the port of entry) that most simulations never touch. And the base year says the designated gateways handle almost none of the affected goods today, which collides with how a linearised CGE model works. Each of the following screens deals with one of these problems in turn.

IThe right shock

TERM gives every import two regional coordinates: where it lands and where it is used. The policy is about the first. The tempting shock is on the second.

Specification

It is an xtrad shock, not a ximps shock

TRADE(c,s,r,d) carries commodity c from region r to region d; for imports, r is the port of entry. ximps(c,d) is imports used in d — shocking it forces the port provinces to consume the nation's imports. xtrad_d(c,"imp",r) is landings by port — the policy variable.

Evidence from trial runs
Use-side trial (national imports fixed, other regions cut 10%): port import use forced up several hundred percent, tariff-equivalent wedges of −55 to −70 percent, port provinces the largest GDP losers in the country (−1.4%), and eventually MAKE<0 — the model refused. Landing-side check (routes to BSK made 5% cheaper via atrad): landings on existing routes +18 to +23 percent, national GDP 0.0000, CPI −0.0001.

Why it matters

Landing and use are different economic events

The landing dimension is not a modelling curiosity; it is where this policy lives, and the base data show it is both real and heavily concentrated:

74%

of electronics imports land in Jakarta; another 19% in Kepulauan Riau. The gateway dimension is real and concentrated.

78%

of electronics imports are used in a different province from the one where they land. Goods pass through gateways; they do not stay.

−1.4%

port-province GDP under the wrong (use-side) shock — the "beneficiaries" become the largest losers as subsidised imports crush local production.

IIThe missing gateways

A percentage-change model cannot grow a cell whose base value is zero. The base year says most BSK gateway routes do not exist.

The problem

Base-year import landings at the three ports

Rp billion. A zero cell has zero weight in every linearised equation — no price, subsidy or preference shock can create a flow that is not in the data. TERM enforces this structurally: routes without flows have no margins either.

This is the central obstacle. Ten of the twenty-one product–port cells are exactly zero and several more are negligible, so for most of the announced goods the gateways do not exist in the model at all. Any simulation of the policy therefore rests on a seeding assumption — a deliberate, documented statement of how much each gateway is assumed to handle initially — and every result inherits that assumption. The fix

Seed upstream: floor the port shares, let the pipeline rebuild

Do not hand-edit the trade matrix — every accounting identity becomes your responsibility. Edit the port-share matrix MSHR in regsupp.har instead; the data pipeline (gravity allocation + RAS) then builds routes, destinations and margins itself, and the standard checks certify the result. One rule, applied by regsupmod.tab:

! floor: each port lands at least Rp 1 billion of each announced product
Formula
 (all,c,POLICY)(all,r,PORTS: V0IMP(c)>1 and MSHR(c,r)*V0IMP(c)<1)
   MSHR(c,r) = 1/V0IMP(c);
! then renormalise every row of port shares to sum to one
 (all,c,COM) TOTS(c) = sum{r,ORG, MSHR(c,r)};
 (all,c,COM: TOTS(c)>0)(all,r,ORG) MSHR(c,r) = MSHR(c,r)/TOTS(c);

A floor, not an addition: cells already above Rp 1 billion are untouched, and renormalisation spreads the offset across existing ports proportionally — no donor port has to be chosen. Both consistency checks (CHKMDA/B) pass with zero failures after the pipeline rerun.

Method detail
V0IMP(c) = national basic-value imports from national.har (imp slices of 1BAS/2BAS/3BAS/5BAS/6BAS); product set resolved through the MCOM mapping in the aggregation file, so it follows any future re-mapping. Workflow: rnregsupmod.bat backs up regsupp.har, runs TABLO + GEMSIM, writes the seeded file in place; then mkdata.bat and doagg rebuild the database. regsupmod.tab and reg1.tab both renormalise, so shares stay valid either way.

IIIExtending the model

To force landings onto a chosen path, the landings variable must be swappable against an instrument. In the standard TAB it is not.

TERM.TAB

A route-cost shifter that enters price and quantity alike

Two obstacles: xtrad is substituted out (cannot be swapped or shocked) and xtrad_d is backsolved (not in the condensed system). Fix: disable the Backsolve, and add fxtrad_d(c,s,r) — uniform over destinations — wherever atrad appears:

! E_puse: the user-price (CES) index over origins
ID01(DELIVRD_R(c,s,d))*puse(c,s,d)
  = sum{r,ORG, DELIVRD(c,s,r,d)*[pdelivrd(c,s,r,d)+atrad(c,s,r,d)+fxtrad_d(c,s,r)]};

! E_xtrad: route demand
xtrad(c,s,r,d) - atrad(c,s,r,d) - fxtrad_d(c,s,r)
  = xuse(c,s,d) + srctwist(c,s,r,d) - avesrctwist(c,s,d)
  - SIGMADOMDOM(c)*[pdelivrd(c,s,r,d)+atrad(c,s,r,d)+fxtrad_d(c,s,r)-puse(c,s,d)];
The price term is not optional

A first version added the shifter to the quantity equation only. It solved — then failed the use-equals-deliveries check (RATB) on the first data update: quantities rationed with no price signal drift away from purchases. Entering exactly like atrad keeps the accounts closed.

And fixes the interpretation

The shifter is an iceberg cost: it burns real resources. GDP losses under this instrument are an upper bound; a rent-generating instrument (a route tax) would show smaller losses.

Closure
Exogenous fxtrad_d; then swap fxtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT) = xtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT); with the landings shock on xtrad_d, and national import volumes held by swap tuser_ud(POLICY,"imp") = natximp(POLICY);. Swap only cells with positive base landings — a zero cell makes the matrix singular.

IVStatics before dynamics

The temptation is to take the modified model straight into RunDynam. With this database, resist it.

Caution

Why RunDynam is not recommended here (yet)

A one-period, comparative-static solve makes every difficulty visible and recoverable: an assertion failure costs seconds, the residual reports whether the solution converged, and each rerun starts from the same audited database. A year-on-year RunDynam sequence has none of these safety nets — and this experiment strains the solver even in its safest form:

Statics already run at the edge

One-period solves need Gragg's method with five subintervals and still sit at accuracy 6 of 10. Rp 1 billion cells absorbing reallocations worth hundreds of billions produce enormous percentage changes — the worst case for a linearised solver.

Dynamics compounds it

Extreme changes recur period after period on an updating database; errors accumulate across years; one assertion failure (MAKE>=0, RATA/RATB) aborts a baserun or policy run midway.

The closure is nonstandard

The fxtrad_d/xtrad_d and tuser_ud/natximp swaps must be carried into every period's closure file. Mistakes are silent until a run dies hours in.

If dynamics is pursued anyway

Aggregating the commodities is a must — fewer, larger cells shrink the multipliers. Also: seed larger initial flows, split the shock across periods, and verify the accounting checks after every period.

VHow far the data lets you push

With Rp 1 billion starting cells, large reallocations are infeasible by construction. The strategy is a search: the largest cut that still solves — one product at a time, then together.

Joint shock

The six products together: the wall is at 15 percent

Uniform cut, west landingsOutcome
−5% / −10% / −15%solves; residual 1.5e−05 to 3.1e−05
−16% to −22%fails: RATB — use ≠ deliveries
−25%fails: RATA — Footwear output at Sorong

The failure modes are economics, not numerics: RATA and RATB are the accounting identities that break when a region is asked to absorb and re-ship more volume than its output and margin capacity allows. Electronics is so extreme — Rp 159 trillion of imports against Rp 3 billion of BSK landings — that no joint cut including it solves at all; it is searched alone on the next screen.

Reading the wall correctly matters. At −15 percent the displaced volume still finds its way through the seeded gateways; one step further and the receiving provinces can no longer absorb and re-ship it — deliveries part company with purchases (RATB), and by −25 percent Sorong's footwear sector would need negative output (RATA). The ceiling is a statement about the assumed size of the gateways, not about the solver.

One product at a time

The largest feasible cut, product by product

Bisection between 1 and 95 percent; a cut is feasible when the solve completes and the maximum residual ratio is below 1e−3. The ceiling scales with existing BSK landings relative to national imports: ceramics starts with Rp 47 billion at the ports and can lose most of its western landings, while electronics starts with Rp 3 billion against Rp 159 trillion of imports and cannot move at all.

Method note
An earlier, looser criterion (completion only) accepted a Cosmetics run whose residual was 1.0 — completion is not convergence; all ceilings above use the strict criterion. Joint-run residual at −15%: 1.48e−05. Scripts: maxcut.sh, maxcut2.sh.

Implications

What this method settles

Specification first

The gateway policy lives on the landing dimension of the trade matrix. A use-side shock produces a different, harsher and wrong experiment. The distinction is invisible in national models — it is the reason to use a TERM-class model at all.

The data is the binding constraint

Zero base landings mean the policy cannot be simulated without a seeding assumption, and every result inherits it. Rp 1 billion per port is deliberately minimal; the feasibility ceilings are its direct consequence.

Seed upstream

Editing the port-share matrix and letting the pipeline rebuild routes, margins and balances is safer than editing trade matrices by hand — the machinery enforces what the modeller would otherwise have to remember.

Assertion failures are findings

MAKE<0 and RATA/RATB failures mark exactly where scenarios stop being economically possible. The model refusing an impossible question is information, not error.
