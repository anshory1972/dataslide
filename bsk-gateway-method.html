The BSK Import Gateways, Simulated

A comparative-static IndoTERM analysis of moving imports to Bitung, Sorong and Kupang

UNPAD & DKEM-BI

The government wants imports of selected goods to enter through Bitung, Sorong and Kupang. Getting there takes three decisions: shock the landing dimension rather than the use dimension, state what each gateway starts with, and extend TERM.TAB so landings can be held on a chosen path. With those settled, all eight scenarios solve at a 95 per cent rerouting — and the second half of the deck reports what they cost and who gains. Browse with PageDown; each screen is one step.

Context

November 2024: move the import gateways east

The Minister of Industry announced that entry points for electronics, textiles and garments, footwear, cosmetics, ceramics and leather goods would move from Java to Sorong, Bitung and Kupang. This deck documents the method: how the policy was made simulable in IndoTERM (23 commodities × 7 regions, the three port provinces kept separate), the traps met on the way, and how large a reallocation the data can support — and, in sections VI and VII, what the eight production runs show and what they imply for the policy.

Three things make this policy unusually demanding to model. It is spatial — a national model cannot even state the question. It concerns a dimension of the data (the port of entry) that most simulations never touch. And the base year says the designated gateways handle almost none of the affected goods today, which collides with how a linearised CGE model works. Each of the following screens deals with one of these problems in turn.

IThe right shock

TERM gives every import two regional coordinates: where it lands and where it is used. The policy is about the first. The tempting shock is on the second.

Specification

It is an xtrad shock, not a ximps shock

TRADE(c,s,r,d) carries commodity c from region r to region d; for imports, r is the port of entry. ximps(c,d) is imports used in d — shocking it forces the port provinces to consume the nation's imports. xtrad_d(c,"imp",r) is landings by port — the policy variable.

Evidence from trial runs
Use-side trial (national imports fixed, other regions cut 10%): port import use forced up several hundred percent, tariff-equivalent wedges of −55 to −70 percent, port provinces the largest GDP losers in the country (−1.4%), and eventually MAKE<0 — the model refused. Landing-side check (routes to BSK made 5% cheaper via atrad): landings on existing routes +18 to +23 percent, national GDP 0.0000, CPI −0.0001.

Why it matters

Landing and use are different economic events

The landing dimension is not a modelling curiosity; it is where this policy lives, and the base data show it is both real and heavily concentrated:

74%

of electronics imports land in Jakarta; another 19% in Kepulauan Riau. The gateway dimension is real and concentrated.

78%

of electronics imports are used in a different province from the one where they land. Goods pass through gateways; they do not stay.

−1.4%

port-province GDP under the wrong (use-side) shock — the "beneficiaries" become the largest losers as subsidised imports crush local production.

IIThe missing gateways

A percentage-change model cannot grow a cell whose base value is zero. The base year says most BSK gateway routes do not exist.

The problem

Base-year import landings at the three ports

Rp billion. A zero cell has zero weight in every linearised equation — no price, subsidy or preference shock can create a flow that is not in the data. TERM enforces this structurally: routes without flows have no margins either.

This is the central obstacle. Ten of the twenty-one product–port cells are exactly zero and several more are negligible, so for most of the announced goods the gateways do not exist in the model at all. Any simulation therefore rests on a seeding assumption — a documented statement of how much each gateway starts with — and every result inherits it. The next screen shows where to place it.

The fix

Seed upstream: set the port shares, let the pipeline rebuild

Do not hand-edit the trade matrix — every accounting identity becomes your responsibility. Edit the port-share matrix MSHR in regsupp.har instead; the data pipeline (gravity allocation + RAS) then builds routes, destinations and margins itself, and the standard checks certify the result. The assumption: each gateway starts with 1 per cent of national landings of each announced good — 3 per cent combined. Applied by regsupmod1pc.tab:

! target share per port, and the raw share that survives renormalisation
Formula
 TARGET = 0.01;
 NPORTS = sum{r,PORTS, 1};
 (all,c,COM) PORTSHR0(c) = sum{r,PORTS, MSHR(c,r)};
 (all,c,COM) SEEDRAW(c)  = TARGET*[1 - PORTSHR0(c)] / [1 - NPORTS*TARGET];
! raise only the ports that start below the target
 (all,c,COM)(all,r,PORTS: SEL(c)>0.5 and MSHR(c,r) < SEEDRAW(c))
   MSHR(c,r) = SEEDRAW(c);
! then renormalise every row of port shares to sum to one
 (all,c,COM) TOTS(c) = sum{r,ORG, MSHR(c,r)};
 (all,c,COM: TOTS(c)>0)(all,r,ORG) MSHR(c,r) = MSHR(c,r)/TOTS(c);

Writing 0.01 directly would not give a 1 per cent share: renormalising afterwards shrinks it. SEEDRAW inverts that step, so the share is exactly 1 per cent after the row is rescaled. It is a floor, not an addition — ports already above the target keep their own value, and no donor port has to be chosen. Both consistency checks (CHKMDA/B) pass with zero failures after the pipeline rerun, and the result is verified against the target to within 0.0002.

Method detail
V0IMP(c) = national basic-value imports from national.har (imp slices of 1BAS/2BAS/3BAS/5BAS/6BAS); product set resolved through the MCOM mapping in the aggregation file, so it follows any future re-mapping. TARGET is a single editable constant. Workflow: rnregsupmod.bat backs up regsupp.har, runs TABLO + GEMSIM, writes the seeded file in place; then mkdata.bat and doagg rebuild the database. regsupmod1pc.tab and reg1.tab both renormalise, so shares stay valid either way.

The pipeline

How the database is built — and where we intercept

mkdata.bat is not one program but a chain: raw tables become the national ORANI-G database and the regional supplement, the regional build turns shares into a full interregional database, and doagg aggregates it for the model. The zero-landing fix is one extra step, inserted where the port shares are born — everything downstream then rebuilds routes, margins and balances automatically.

Reading the chart
Stage 1 (folder tpmh0198) builds national.har from the input tables and derives regsupp.har, whose MSHR header holds each product's import shares by port of entry — the zeros live here. Stage 2 is the one added step: rnregsupmod.bat backs up regsupp.har and runs regsupmod1pc.tab, which raises MSHR using national import values from national.har and the product mapping from the aggregation file, then renormalises. Stage 3 (folder tpmh0197) consumes the seeded shares: reg1 reads MSHR, the gravity and RAS steps construct the interregional trade and margin matrices, premod assembles the 185×34 master database. Stage 4 aggregates to 23×7 and chkmod certifies every accounting identity. Because the seed enters upstream, no identity is ever edited by hand.

IIIExtending the model

To force landings onto a chosen path, the landings variable must be swappable against an instrument. In the standard TAB it is not.

TERM.TAB

A route-cost shifter that enters price and quantity alike

Two obstacles: xtrad is substituted out (cannot be swapped or shocked) and xtrad_d is backsolved (not in the condensed system). Fix: disable the Backsolve, and add fxtrad_d(c,s,r) — uniform over destinations — wherever atrad appears:

! E_puse: the user-price (CES) index over origins
ID01(DELIVRD_R(c,s,d))*puse(c,s,d)
  = sum{r,ORG, DELIVRD(c,s,r,d)*[pdelivrd(c,s,r,d)+atrad(c,s,r,d)+fxtrad_d(c,s,r)]};

! E_xtrad: route demand
xtrad(c,s,r,d) - atrad(c,s,r,d) - fxtrad_d(c,s,r)
  = xuse(c,s,d) + srctwist(c,s,r,d) - avesrctwist(c,s,d)
  - SIGMADOMDOM(c)*[pdelivrd(c,s,r,d)+atrad(c,s,r,d)+fxtrad_d(c,s,r)-puse(c,s,d)];
The price term is not optional

A first version added the shifter to the quantity equation only. It solved — then failed the use-equals-deliveries check (RATB) on the first data update: quantities rationed with no price signal drift away from purchases. Entering exactly like atrad keeps the accounts closed.

And fixes the interpretation

The shifter is an iceberg cost: it burns real resources. GDP losses under this instrument are an upper bound; a rent-generating instrument (a route tax) would show smaller losses.

Closure
Exogenous fxtrad_d; then swap fxtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT) = xtrad_d(POLICY,"imp",WCUT); with the landings shock on xtrad_d, and national import volumes held by swap tuser_ud(POLICY,"imp") = natximp(POLICY);. Swap only cells with positive base landings — a zero cell makes the matrix singular.

IVStatics before dynamics

The temptation is to take the modified model straight into RunDynam. With this database, resist it.

Caution

Why RunDynam is not recommended here (yet)

A one-period, comparative-static solve makes every difficulty visible and recoverable: an assertion failure costs seconds, the residual reports whether the solution converged, and each rerun starts from the same audited database. A year-on-year RunDynam sequence has none of these safety nets — and this experiment strains the solver even in its safest form:

Statics already run at the edge

One-period solves need Gragg's method with five subintervals and still sit at accuracy 6 of 10. Seeded gateway cells absorbing reallocations many times their own size produce percentage changes in the thousands — landings at the three ports rise by 1,700 to 4,300 per cent. That is the worst case for a linearised solver.

Dynamics compounds it

Extreme changes recur period after period on an updating database; errors accumulate across years; one assertion failure (MAKE>=0, RATA/RATB) aborts a baserun or policy run midway.

The closure is nonstandard

The fxtrad_d/xtrad_d and tuser_ud/natximp swaps must be carried into every period's closure file. Mistakes are silent until a run dies hours in.

If dynamics is pursued anyway

Aggregating the commodities is a must — fewer, larger cells shrink the multipliers. Also: seed larger initial flows, split the shock across periods, and verify the accounting checks after every period.

VHow far the data lets you push

Before running a policy scenario, find out what the database will bear. The strategy is a search: the largest cut that still solves — one product at a time, then all seven together.

The search

Every product solves at a 95 per cent cut — and so do all seven at once

Bisection between 1 and 95 per cent, one product at a time and then jointly. A cut counts as feasible only when the solve completes and the maximum residual ratio is below 1e−3 — completion is not convergence. Every search reached the ceiling of its range: nothing binds below 95 per cent, and −99 also solves. These eight runs are the production set.

Implications

What this method settles

Specification first

The gateway policy lives on the landing dimension of the trade matrix. A use-side shock produces a different, harsher and wrong experiment. The distinction is invisible in national models — it is the reason to use a TERM-class model at all.

The data is the binding constraint

Zero base landings mean the policy cannot be simulated without a seeding assumption, and every result inherits it. Here each gateway is assumed to start with 1 per cent of national landings of each announced good. Report that assumption beside every number it produces.

Seed upstream

Editing the port-share matrix and letting the pipeline rebuild routes, margins and balances is safer than editing trade matrices by hand — the machinery enforces what the modeller would otherwise have to remember.

Assertion failures are findings

MAKE<0 and RATA/RATB failures mark exactly where scenarios stop being economically possible. The model refusing an impossible question is information, not error.

Results · consumer prices

Prices rise everywhere — least where the goods now land

Rerouting makes imported goods more expensive to deliver to the places where most people live, so consumer prices rise in every region — nationally +2.46 percent in the combined run. Results · the port economies

What grows in BSK: the logistics complex, then electronics

VIIHighlights and recommendations

What else the eight runs reveal, the caveats they carry, and what they imply for how the government should stage the policy.

Highlights

Six things the headline numbers do not show

Electronics is the whole ballgame — in both directions

It causes 86 percent of the national GDP loss (−2.63 of −3.05), and it is also the only product whose rerouting delivers the port provinces a visible boom. Decisions about this policy are, in practice, decisions about electronics.

The six manufactures are a different policy

Each costs at most 0.11 percent of GDP, moves port GDP by less than a third of a percentage point — yet port households gain in all six runs, because goods landing locally cut local prices. Small cost, small but real local benefit.

The trade account takes the hit

National export volume falls 10.0 percent in the combined run and electronics exports effectively stop (−98). Meanwhile total import volume rises 1.33 percent even though all seven policy commodities are held fixed — the economy substitutes toward other imports as domestic costs climb.

The hidden price tag: a 17–55 percent wedge

To make importers actually reroute, the solution requires imports through BSK to become 53–55 percent cheaper for the six manufactures and 17 percent cheaper for electronics (the tuser_ud wedge) — the size of the logistics subsidy, or cost saving, the policy implicitly assumes.

Jobs move east, but shrink in total

National employment falls 1.77 percent. Port-province employment jumps — PapuaBar +9.5, NTT +5.4, SulUt +4.8 percent — while Jawa loses 2.5 percent. The policy relocates more jobs than it creates.

Two caveats travel with every number

The instrument is an iceberg cost, so the GDP losses are an upper bound; a rent-generating instrument would lose less. And everything is conditional on the 1 percent seed: gateways able to handle that traffic must exist. Nothing here says they can be built, or what building them would cost.

1 · Stage the rollout by product

Move the six light manufactures first: the national cost is small (≤0.11 percent of GDP each) and port households benefit immediately. Hold electronics back until the gateways demonstrably handle the easier products.

2 · State the trade-off honestly

This is regional redistribution bought with national income: roughly Rp 69 of real GDP lost elsewhere for every Rp 1 gained in the port provinces (contributions: ports +0.045 points of the national −3.05). If the objective is eastern development, compare this price with direct alternatives — infrastructure grants, transfers — before committing.

3 · Build the ports before the mandate

Every favourable result assumes each gateway already handles 1 percent of national landings of each good — capacity that does not exist today, since the base year records almost no landings of these goods at the three ports. Port investment is not a complement to this policy; it is its precondition.

4 · If electronics moves, move it partially

A near-total electronics rerouting costs 2.63 percent of GDP and shuts down electronics exports. Cap the rerouted share at what port capacity supports and revisit as capacity grows, rather than mandating a full shift at once.

5 · Attack the wedge, watch the prices

The policy implicitly requires BSK routes to be 17–55 percent cheaper. Every rupiah spent lowering real port and shipping costs shrinks that wedge and the national loss with it. Monitor CPI region by region: Jawa (+2.6 percent) bears the largest consumer-price burden.

6 · Treat these as bounding estimates

Iceberg costs make the losses an upper bound; the 1 percent seed makes the port gains conditional on infrastructure. The honest summary for cabinet: small, real gains for three provinces; a measurable cost for the nation; electronics decides the size of both.
