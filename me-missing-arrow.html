Why it binds without a law

The Treasury approves spending above each department’s delegated limit only on a business case written to the Green Book. The Accounting Officer answers to Parliament for value for money; the National Audit Office audits against the same books.

Green Book 1.3

“It is mandatory for UK government departments and arm’s length bodies to follow the Green Book ... when developing proposals that involve significant public spending.”

Green Book 2.3

Green Book: rationale, objectives, appraisal. Teal Book: delivery. Magenta Book: monitoring, evaluation, feedback.

One ministry, one series

HM Treasury is finance ministry and budget authority in one. The guidance on how to justify money comes from the office that releases it.

Part 2 · A framework that names the step

The ROAMEF cycle: three steps before delivery, three after

Rationale
Why does the government need to act?
Objectives
What exactly are the intended outcomes?
Appraisal
What are the options? How do they compare?
Monitoring

The presenter’s deck covers the three steps after delivery begins. The three before it are where MBG and KDMP went wrong.

Part 2 · A framework that names the step

Rationale and objectives: five things written down before any option is costed

Case for change

Why government must act, and why households, firms and charities cannot do it themselves. Market failure or equity. (4.2–4.7)

Theory of change

Inputs, outputs, outcomes, with the assumptions stated. Based on evidence, not asserted. (4.9–4.13)

Business as usual

What happens with no new intervention. The benchmark for every option. Deadweight versus additionality. (4.14–4.19)

Objectives

SMART. Who benefits, how many, where, by when. Outcomes rather than outputs. (4.20–4.26)

Strategic fit

Fits the programme above it and the work of other public bodies. (4.27)

“A theory of change should not simply assert that the specified inputs will lead to the intended outcomes.” (4.11)

Evidence from previous evaluations, research and international comparisons “should take place before detailed business case development begins.” (4.13)

Source & method
HM Treasury, The Green Book (2026), chapter 4, paragraphs 4.1 to 4.27 and Table 1. Paragraph numbers in brackets.

Part 2 · A framework that names the step

Appraisal: three stages, and the pilot is written into each of them

1 · Options and longlistCritical success factors. A wide longlist of ways to meet the objective, including other instruments. Shortlist of about five, always with business as usual and do minimum. (5.1–5.20)
2 · Shortlist appraisalSocial cost-benefit or cost-effectiveness analysis. Whole-life, discounted, with risk, optimism bias and distribution. (ch. 6)
3 · Preferred optionA balanced value-for-money judgement, not the highest benefit-cost ratio alone. Appraisal summary table. Evaluation plan agreed and costed before approval. (2.29–2.32, 10.7–10.8)
Where the pilot sitsImplementation is one of five option choices: “an initial pilot, a phased rollout or a rapid implementation approach.” (5.25)
Where the pilot sitsUncertainty from a weak rationale “can be reduced by research, evaluations of previous interventions and pilot studies.” (6.63)
Where the pilot sitsThe Strategic Business Case must include “a discussion of the scope for piloting and testing prior to implementation.” (Magenta Book, Fig. 1.3)

Options generation and longlist appraisal “are often overlooked ... Practitioners are often tempted to skip these stages” (5.3). A programme that arrives as a single option has skipped Appraisal, not just Monitoring.

Source & method
HM Treasury, The Green Book (2026), Table 1 and chapters 5, 6 and 10; Magenta Book (2026), sections 1.4-1.5 and Figure 1.3.

What she said, 8 August 2025

MBG “dapat menjadi sarana” for learning: children can learn to count and meet English words through the foods served, if packaged creatively. No study was named.

What ANTARA wrote

“Wamen Stella: MBG berpotensi tingkatkan kemampuan matematika dan bahasa Inggris”

Assessment

The hedged claim is defensible. The headline is not what she said. Nobody in the exchange cited the evidence that exists.

Claim 2: “MBG cannot lower poverty because the state pays for it”

“Makan bergizi gratis itu tidak masuk dalam pengeluaran rumah tangga karena yang membayar negara.”

Nailul Huda, Director, CELIOS, to Bisnis, 28 April 2026

How Susenas records a meal eaten at school

Block 4.1 asks for food consumed but not bought. The enumerator values it “dengan harga setempat” and writes it in column K8.

Where it goes

K8 flows into FOOD, then EXPEND, then KAPITA, the variable BPS uses for the poverty line. The 8.25% of September 2025 already contains the meals.

So the objection is a factual error

In-kind food is household consumption by construction. The right question is how much the meals are worth and who gets them.

Part 3 · Rationale under fire

The pattern in all three: the hedge is lost, the evidence arrives late

1 · A hedged claim

“dapat”, “bisa”, “berpotensi”, “jika implementasi baik”. Three officials, three true sentences.

2 · The headline drops the hedge

“MBG bisa tingkatkan matematika”; “MBG turunkan kemiskinan 5,8%”; “Rp10 ribu cukup”.

3 · The critic attacks the headline

with a counter-claim that is itself unmeasured: “halu”, “not household spending”, “nothing”.

4 · Nobody cites the rationale

because none was published. The evidence existed: a Cochrane review, Susenas, a one-line calculation.

A Rationale written down before launch is not bureaucracy. It is the document you hold up when the headline is wrong. Without it, the evidence enters the debate only after the programme has been attacked.

Part 4 · Appraisal in practice: the DPI pilot

The theory of change of the DPI pilot, and the assumption under each step

1
Register
The household applies on the Perlinsos Portal, alone or with an agent
2
Verify
Answers checked against Dukcapil, PLN, BPJS, Korlantas, ATR/BPN through SPLP
3
Update
DTSEN becomes dynamic: the decile is recomputed from current data
4
Rank
A short formula of 8–9 verifiable variables
5
Decide and appeal
Eligibility for every programme from one record; complaints change the record
6
Pay
Every cash benefit to an account in the recipient's name

Assumption behind step 1

Eligible households register, and ineligible recipients do not. Testable: who registered in Bali, by decile.

Assumption behind steps 2 and 4

Administrative records (BPJS wage, car, land, electricity) separate deciles 1–4 from 5–10. Testable: whom each filter removes.

Assumption behind step 3

A decile recomputed from current data is closer to the truth than the 2022-based DTSEN decile. Testable: against the Susenas decile.

What the pilot adds

Bali, May–July 2026, every kabupaten/kota. BPS linked registrants to 6,127 Susenas households by NIK, so each assumption gets a number.

Closing

Three asks for the M&E strategy

AskWhat it meansROAMEF stage
1. No national rollout without an appraisalThe programme document names the alternatives considered, the business-as-usual benchmark, and the pilot or phased-rollout decision with reasons (Green Book 5.4, 5.25, 5.27).Appraisal
2. The M&E plan is part of the decision, not an afterthoughtWritten and costed before approval, drawn from the theory of change; the rollout order chosen so that a comparison group exists (Green Book 10.7–10.8; Magenta Book 1.6).Objectives → Monitoring
3. A public evaluation registryEvery planned, live and completed evaluation of a central programme registered and published, so the next rationale can cite the last evaluation (Green Book 10.11).Feedback → Rationale

The deck asks for better monitoring. These three asks would give it something worth monitoring.
