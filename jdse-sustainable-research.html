A journal’s presence is decided by three choices: which conversation it joins, who it is for, and what it does that the incumbents do not. The first two are measurable. The third is a design decision, and the last slide proposes one.

Part I · Where is the field going?

Method: how the field was measured

1 · Corpus

OpenAlex works, 2010–2026, articles and reviews. No keyword matching: papers are selected by the topic OpenAlex assigns to every work (the same taxonomy SCImago uses).

2 · Six sustainable topics

Sustainable finance and green bonds; climate-change policy and economics; sustainability and climate governance; sustainable development and environmental policy, and management; CSR and ESG reporting. 543,190 works carry at least one of them; 181,067 have one as primary topic, 2016–2026.

3 · Five digital topics

FinTech, crowdfunding and digital finance; digital transformation in financial services; microfinance and financial inclusion; blockchain; digital economy and transformation. 398,142 works.

4 · The intersection

Works that carry a topic from both sets: 5,143 works. This is the literature JDSE is named for.

5 · Themes

OpenAlex keywords within the sustainable-finance topic, 2015–2018 against 2023–2026, as shares of the topic’s output.

6 · Four lenses

Every journal matched by ISSN to the Scopus Source List of September 2026 and to SCImago 2025. Each result is reported for all OpenAlex journals, all Scopus-listed journals, Scopus excluding discontinued titles, and Scopus Q1 excluding discontinued titles.

Source & method
OpenAlex topic IDs: T13146, T10471, T10119, T12013, T12883, T10115 (sustainable); T11995, T13346, T10987, T10270, T12958 (digital). Filter-only queries with server-side group_by; no text search.

Part I · Where is the field going?

Figure 2 · The themes moved from climate finance to climate risk, and the Q1 journals moved furthest

Same direction under both lenses

Climate finance falls (10% to 6% in all journals; 12% to 5% in Q1). Climate risk, transition risk, ESG ratings and greenwashing rise in both.

Q1 is more about risk and markets

In Q1 journals climate risk is 9% of recent papers, against 7% overall; financial stability 4% against 3%.

Q1 is less about green finance as a label

Green finance carries 36% of recent papers overall but 31% in Q1: the label is most common where the review bar is lowest.

The gap

An non-discontinued Scopus journal holds 47% of the world’s papers on these themes and 8% of Indonesia’s. Remove CSR reporting and Indonesia rises to 17%.

Where Indonesia’s Q1 work goes

The same international titles the world uses: no Indonesian journal appears among them.

The implication

The authors exist and are productive. What they lack is a venue that is both reachable and indexed. That is the author base JDSE can serve, if it holds the line on quality.

Implications

What the evidence implies for JDSE

1 · Position at the intersection

Digital and sustainable finance together: 4,640 papers in all journals, 1,977 in Q1, growing faster than anything else under every lens (Figures 1 and 3), and no home journal. Claim it in the title, the aims and the first call.

2 · Follow the themes that rose in Q1

Climate and transition risk, financial stability, ESG ratings and greenwashing, green credit (Figure 2). These are supervisory questions and they rose most in the Q1 journals; a central bank is the natural host.

3 · Serve the emerging-market author

Indonesia is 2nd in the world by papers in all journals and 32th in Q1 (Figures 10 to 12). The authors exist; the venue is missing. ASEAN-6 is the first author base; India, Pakistan and Vietnam are where the institution-hosted journals found theirs (Figure 8).

4 · Be diamond, and be indexed

No fee to read or publish, on a Scopus track from the first issue: 28% of the field is diamond but 2% of Q1 is (Figure 9). Institution-hosted journals reached Q1 in zero to three years; DOAJ in year one, Scopus application after two years of regular issues (Figure 7).

5 · Require data and code, publish briefs

Every empirical paper with replication files; a two-page policy brief alongside. Few journals in the field do either. It is cheap for a host that already has analysts.

6 · Measure from day one, under the Q1 lens

Citations, author countries, topic mix and index status, from OpenAlex, on a public page. BMEB’s h-index of 33 and two citations per paper in two years is the baseline to beat, and the number to report against.

Sources

Sources and limits

OpenAlex works, topics, keywords, sources and institutions, retrieved 14 October 2026: 248,756 works with a sustainable primary topic and 398,142 with a digital topic, 2010-2026, downloaded one by one so that every journal could be classified. Journal status from the official Scopus Source List, September 2026 (sources sheet and discontinued-titles sheet), matched by ISSN and EISSN; quartiles from SCImago Journal Rank 2025. Scopus Search API (Elsevier), 14 October 2026, phrase set in TITLE-ABS-KEY, for the cross-check. Method follows the Indonesian research-quality study (anshory1972.github.io/dataslide/researchquality.html): classification by assigned topic, not keyword matching; journal status by ISSN; repositories separated from journals; every result reported under four lenses.

Limits. OpenAlex is a reliable census of the literature and less reliable on who wrote it; institution counts are indicative. Topic assignment is probabilistic. Keyword coverage is thinner before 2020, so early-period shares are lower bounds. 2026 is a partial year. A journal is matched to Scopus only if its ISSN is in the list, which undercounts by a few per cent. Figures 4 to 8 use the 200 largest venues of each group; Figures 1 to 3 and 9 to 13 use every paper.
