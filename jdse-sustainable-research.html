A journal’s presence is decided by three choices: which conversation it joins, who it is for, and what it does that the incumbents do not. The first two are measurable. The third is a design decision, and the last slide proposes one.

Part I · Where is the field going?

Method: how the field was measured

1 · Corpus

OpenAlex works, 2010–2026, articles and reviews. No keyword matching: papers are selected by the topic OpenAlex assigns to every work (the same taxonomy SCImago uses).

2 · Six sustainable topics

Sustainable finance and green bonds; climate-change policy and economics; sustainability and climate governance; sustainable development and environmental policy, and management; CSR and ESG reporting. 543,190 works carry at least one of them; 181,067 have one as primary topic, 2016–2026.

3 · Five digital topics

FinTech, crowdfunding and digital finance; digital transformation in financial services; microfinance and financial inclusion; blockchain; digital economy and transformation. 398,142 works.

4 · The intersection

Works that carry a topic from both sets: 5,143 works. This is the literature JDSE is named for.

5 · Themes

OpenAlex keywords within the sustainable-finance topic, 2015–2018 against 2023–2026, as shares of the topic’s output.

6 · Cross-check

Scopus Search API, a phrase set (sustainable finance, green finance, green bonds, climate finance, ESG investing, transition risk, carbon pricing, green credit), counts by year, world and Indonesia.

Source & method
OpenAlex topic IDs: T13146, T10471, T10119, T12013, T12883, T10115 (sustainable); T11995, T13346, T10987, T10270, T12958 (digital). Filter-only queries with server-side group_by; no text search.

Part I · Where is the field going?

Figure 2 · The themes moved from climate finance to climate risk, from public money to firms, banks and ratings

Fading

Climate finance (17% to 6%) and climate-change mitigation (8% to 4%): the public-finance and NDC framing of the 2015 Paris years has given way to markets and firms.

Rising

Climate risk (4% to 7%), transition risk and ESG ratings (from nothing), greenwashing (1% to 4%), green credit (2% to 4%), financial stability (1% to 3%): the central-bank and supervisory agenda is now inside the topic.

Geography

China rose from 2% to 7% of the topic; emerging markets from 1% to 4%. The empirical centre of the field moved east and south.

Source & method
OpenAlex keywords on works whose primary topic is Sustainable finance and green bonds: 1,908 works in 2015-2018, 16,265 in 2023-2026. A work can carry several keywords. Keyword assignment is less complete for older works, so early shares are lower bounds.

Part I · Where is the field going?

Figure 3 · The digital-sustainable intersection is small, fast and has no home journal

What it is

Green bonds and fintech; digital finance and the energy transition; blockchain for carbon markets and ESG data; financial inclusion and climate resilience. Still only 5,143 papers in sixteen years, 1,479 in 2025.

Where it goes now

Its venues are the generalists: Sustainability, Finance Research Letters, Environmental Science and Pollution Research. No journal is built for it.

What that means

The intersection is small, growing at 30–40% a year, and homeless. That is the definition of a niche a new journal can own.

Source & method
OpenAlex works carrying at least one topic from each set, 2010-2026. Venue list from the same query grouped by source.

The 90%

Nine in ten Indonesian papers on these themes are in journals Scopus does not index: university accounting journals, Owner, COSTING, KnE proceedings. Correct for the CSR topic and it is still seven in ten.

The Scopus view

Scopus itself sees 176 Indonesian papers in 2025 for the phrase set, 3.2% of the world; ASEAN-6 481, 8.6%.

The implication

The authors exist and are productive. What they lack is a venue that is both reachable and indexed. That is the author base JDSE can serve, if it holds the line on quality.

Source & method
Same venue classification as Figure 5, applied to works with an Indonesian author (all six topics; and excluding CSR and ESG reporting) and to ASEAN-6 (Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Singapore). Scopus cross-check: TITLE-ABS-KEY phrase set with AFFILCOUNTRY.

Implications

What the evidence implies for JDSE

1 · Position at the intersection

Digital and sustainable finance together is a 5,000-paper literature growing 35% a year with no home journal (Figure 3). Claim it in the title, the aims and the first call.

2 · Follow the themes that rose

Climate and transition risk, green credit, financial stability, ESG ratings and greenwashing, carbon pricing (Figure 2). These are supervisory questions; a central bank is the natural host.

3 · Serve the emerging-market author

Indonesia is second in the world by count and 90% invisible (Figures 10 and 12). ASEAN-6 is the first author base; India, Pakistan and Vietnam are where Borsa Istanbul Review and Financial Innovation found theirs (Figure 8).

4 · Be diamond, and be indexed

No fee to read or publish, on a Scopus track from the first issue (Figure 9). The institution-hosted models reached Q1 in three years; DOAJ in year one, Scopus application after two years of regular issues (Figure 7).

5 · Require data and code, publish briefs

Every empirical paper with replication files; a two-page policy brief alongside. Few journals in the field do either. It is cheap for a host that already has analysts.

6 · Measure from day one

Citations, author countries, topic mix and index status, from OpenAlex, on a public page. BMEB’s h 33 and two citations per paper in two years is the baseline to beat, and the number to report against.
