SDGs Center Business Model · Final Report, 2025

SDGs Alignment of Indonesia’s Research Strength

Arief Anshory Yusuf

Indonesia has built 62 SDG Centers in a decade. Whether they are pointed at the right problems depends on two things the study had to measure separately: what universities are strong at, and what provinces actually need. This section sets out how each side was measured, before turning to what the numbers show.

IHow the study was done

Supply and demand are measured from entirely different sources — bibliometric databases on one side, planning documents and official indicators on the other. The gap analysis only makes sense once both are expressed as a ranking over the same 16 goals.

The evidence base

A mixed-methods study, with the quantitative work resting on four data sources

The evidence base

Four strands

  1. Desk studyRPJMD content analysis for all 34 provinces, bibliometrics from SciVal and Scopus, and a literature review on multidisciplinary research centres.
  2. SurveysProvincial Bappeda on SDG implementation and needs; SDG Centers on their institutional arrangements.
  3. FGDsLocal governments, universities and operating SDG Centers, plus the ministry, to align programmes with the university resource map.
  4. QuantitativeThe Bappenas SDG Dashboard, used to align provincial progress with university research strengths.

Survey coverage

27 of 34 provinces responded

Fieldwork ran 25 February to 14 March 2025. Coverage is strong everywhere except the east.

Supply

Two bibliometric layers, merged

Research strength is measured at the university, then attributed to the province the university sits in.

  1. SciVal — quality181 Indonesian institutions listed; 21 excluded as non-higher-education, leaving 160 universities. Metric: citation count per SDG.
  2. Scopus — quantity578 Indonesian organisations, of which 457 are universities. Metric: number of documents contributing to each SDG.
  3. Concordance455 Scopus institution names matched semi-manually against the 160 SciVal names, because naming varies across Indonesian and English forms and abbreviation styles.
Source & method
Report §2 and Table 2-1. SciVal covers 160 of the 457 universities Scopus lists, and coverage is uneven across provinces — from 31 universities in East Java to none in seven provinces.

Two definitions

Demand is measured twice, and the two measures do not have to agree

Demand, first definition

What provinces say they want

Content analysis of the medium-term development plan (RPJMD) of all 34 provinces.

  1. ExtractVision, mission, goals and priority programmes from each province's RPJMD.
  2. Map to SDGsManual review by the research team, then ChatGPT as a comparative tool, then verification by senior researchers.
  3. RankGoals ordered by prominence; the highest priority is rank 1.
Source & method
Most documents pre-date the 2024 regional elections, since new RPJMDs were still being drafted. The report argues continuity is reasonable because every RPJMD must follow the longer-horizon RPJPD and the national RPJMN.

Demand, second definition

Where provinces are actually falling short

An achievement index built from the National SDGs Secretariat's indicator set.

  1. NormaliseEach of 285 indicator-dimensions rescaled 0–1 against official thresholds, or empirical bounds (±2 SD) where no standard exists, with direction set by whether higher or lower is better.
  2. AggregatePrincipal component analysis within each goal, producing a 0–100 achievement index per goal per province.
  3. RankGoals 1–16 ordered by index; the lowest score is rank 1, the goal most in need of support.
Source & method
This is the demand measure used in the report's gap analysis. The two definitions can disagree: a province may rank a goal low in its plan while performing badly on it.

What the achievement index rests on

The indicator base is very uneven across goals

Number of indicator-dimensions available per SDG in the provincial dataset. Goal 4 has 117; Goals 12 and 14 have one each.

Source & method
Report Table 2-2, 285 indicator-dimensions in total. A goal measured by a single indicator carries the same weight in the ranking as one measured by 117, which is worth keeping in mind when reading the demand side.

Putting the two sides together

From supply and demand to a gap

Two tests, both run province by province and for Indonesia as a whole.

  1. Spearman rank correlationCorrelates the ranking of SDGs by university output against the ranking of SDGs by achievement. Because rank 1 means ‘most published’ on one side and ‘worst performing’ on the other, a positive coefficient means research is concentrated where the province is weakest.
  2. Top-5 matchingCounts how many goals appear in both a province's five strongest SDGs by publication and its five weakest by achievement.
Source & method
Report §4, Tables 4-21, 4-22 and 4-23.
