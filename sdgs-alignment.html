SDGs Alignment of Indonesia’s Research Strength

Arief Anshory Yusuf

Indonesia’s universities publish a great deal on the SDGs. The question is whether they publish on the goals their own provinces are failing.

Method

How alignment was measured

Supply and demand are ranked over the same 16 goals for each of the 34 provinces, then compared.

  1. SupplyScopus publication counts per SDG for 457 Indonesian universities, attributed to the province the university sits in. Each province's five strongest goals are its supply.
  2. Measured demand285 official indicator-dimensions from the National SDGs Secretariat, normalised and aggregated by principal component analysis into a 0–100 achievement index per goal. Each province's five weakest goals are its demand.
  3. Stated demandA survey of provincial Bappeda fielded 25 February – 14 March 2025 and answered by 27 of the 34 provinces — agency heads, division leaders and planners — setting out each province's own declared priorities, implementation constraints and existing university partnerships. Complemented by focus group discussions with local governments, universities and operating SDG Centers, and by content analysis of all 34 provincial RPJMDs.
  4. AlignmentA goal on both the supply and demand lists is a match — a development gap the province's own universities are already strong in.
Source & method
SDG Center Business Model final report (2025), §2, Table 4-22 and Figure 4-1. Figure 1 carries both demand measures; Figure 2 carries only the revealed one, because the survey has no per-province breakdown. Supply is measured by university location, not by where the research is about.

Method

How research quality was measured

978,546 journal articles and reviews indexed to Indonesian institutions on OpenAlex, 2015–2026, with no topic filter, cross-classified against Scopus.

  1. Journal qualityEvery source matched by ISSN against the Scopus CiteScore quartile list, May 2026 edition — Q1 through Q4, delisted, indexed-but-unranked, or not indexed at all.
  2. Coverage checkOpenAlex's journal register was tested directly against Elsevier's own API: 250 randomly drawn “not in Scopus” journals came back zero false negatives. OpenAlex is a reliable census of the literature.
  3. Institution checkNot a reliable authority on who wrote it. Sampling raw affiliation strings against each institution's own name found three of the 25 largest producer records — sharing name templates like IAIN X or STKIP X — conflating an entire class of similarly-named institutions. Established universities (UGM, IPB, USU) matched at 92–99%; the three broken records matched at 1–6%. All three are excluded below.
  4. RankingThe 25 largest remaining producers, ranked twice over the same period: once by total output, once by Q1-only output.
Source & method
OpenAlex works metadata (2015–2026), Scopus CiteScore quartile list (May 2026), and the Scopus Sources API, used to verify OpenAlex's journal register directly.
