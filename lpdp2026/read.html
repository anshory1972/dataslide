1 · The old paradigm

Grow first, fix equality and the environment later

For decades, the standard view was that a poor country must accept a trade-off. Up to a turning point, higher income comes with lower equality and a worse environment. Only beyond the turning point can both improve.

2 · The arguments

Old arguments for the trade-off, and why each one fails

Growth requires a trade-offThe old claim. The arguments for it do not hold.
Equality: growth was said to require inequality
Growth depends mainly on capital and saving
Returns to capital diminish. Growth comes from innovation, which needs broad-based human capital.
The gains will trickle down to the poor
This rarely happens. Redistribution is a policy choice, and elites often block it.
Growth is always good for the poor
Not always. Growth can leave the poor worse off (immiserizing growth).
Leave it to the marketThatcherism, Reaganomics, 1980s
Inequality rose, with its social costs, and support for the far right grew.
Efficiency first, minimum governmentstructural reform
A simple view of markets. Many countries implemented Washington Consensus reforms in the 1990s, and growth was disappointing.
Environment: growth was said to require environmental damage
Countries must industrialize first
Industry without controls creates external costs, and these reduce growth.
Urbanize first, manage later
Unmanaged urbanization lowers labour productivity.
The environment is a luxury
Pollution damages health, and health is not a luxury.
Poor countries lack the regulation
Rules fail because interest groups block them.
Poor countries lack the resources
Not when the full cost of the damage is counted.
Rich countries polluted first; now it is our turn
Our goals can match: energy security through renewable energy, as China shows.

1 · Foundational

Essential, for everyone

Basic functions at the scale of the whole society, used across sectors. Can be built or run by the government, by firms, or both.

2 · Building blocks

Reused, not rebuilt

Interoperable, open standards and open APIs, so many services use the same block. No more central data than needed.

3 · Public benefit

Safe and inclusive by design

Inclusion and user choice, privacy by design, strong governance with public oversight, built in from the start.

Why it matters

Four services built separately: 12 systems, records that do not match. Built on DPI: 3 shared blocks, one record per person.

Potential benefits
  • Government: faster, cheaper services
  • Firms: more competition and innovation
  • People: easier access, more control
Risks to manage
  • Exclusion of people with low digital access or skills
  • Misuse of personal data
  • Lock-in, poor-quality systems, weak capacity

1
A funding decision

Budget: fewer places than eligible households

The quota is set before eligibility. PKH: about 20 m households potentially eligible, 10 m places. Sembako: 26 m eligible, 18.3 m places. Those left out count as exclusion error.

2
DPI

Business process: the money does not arrive cleanly

Payment problems · hard-to-reach areas · leakage and corruption · elite capture of who gets listed.

3
DPI

Wrong deciles: the PMT formula misclassifies

Welfare y is not observed, so a proxy means test (PMT) predicts it from household characteristics x.

y = ŷ + e = f(x) + e

Households are ranked by ŷ alone. But x is old (Regsosek 2022) and inaccurate, and e, what x cannot explain, moves households across the cut-off.

What e includes: unobserved factors that shape welfare
online loans (pinjol)lottery prizesillnessdisastersinheritanceremittances charity receivedremote locationaccidents
70%misplaced among 10 deciles
50%misplaced among 5 groups
20%misplaced between 2 groups

DPI answer: current, verified x from administrative data through SPLP.

How DPI improves targeting: where it acts, from registration to payment

Registration

People register themselves, or with help, to enter and update their data.

On-demand application: self-registration or assisted
DPI Self-targeting: reduces inclusion and exclusion error (Alatas et al., 2017)

Data updating and integration

Welfare status is imputed; data from other agencies are pulled through SPLP.

PMT predictor and SPLP, with administrative data from Dukcapil, Korlantas, ATR/BPN, BPJS-TK, PLN, BKN, telco and SLIK/OJK
DPI Ground check: fewer home visits
DPI Direct pull / verification: more accurate data
DPI Cross-validation: checks across data sources

Social registry and PMT

An integrated, dynamic database and a PMT model classify households by welfare.

DTSEN (Data Tunggal Sosial Ekonomi Nasional), PMT model, decile classification
DPI Dynamic social registry: welfare status more current than census results
DPI Less misclassification: more accurate deciles

Eligibility decision

The decile and additional criteria decide who is eligible. The decile is not the only criterion, but it usually dominates.

Eligibility decision, additional (non-decile) criteria from administrative data

Grievance and registry

Appeals correct the data; graduation status is recorded.

Beneficiary registry, grievance (sanggah)
DPI Grievance update: administrative data corrected

Digital payment (G2P)

The NIK is mapped to an account; assistance is paid directly.

Central mapper (NIK ↔ account, real time), G2P payment

DPI Payment readiness: interoperable systems

15 · Reflection

Reflection: why do we need to improve social protection?

Does low productivity create poverty, or does poverty create low productivity?

A modern state protects its people. Social protection that reaches the right households, quickly, is a basic task of the state.
Social protection is an investment in growth. People are less productive because they are poor, not only the other way round. The evidence is below.
Do not tax the poor with paperwork. Long forms and interviews use up the mental capacity of the poor (p. 980). Simple digital registration avoids this.

The same farmer thinks less clearly when he is poor

464 small sugarcane farmers in 54 villages, Tamil Nadu, India, tested twice in 2010: before and after the harvest payment.

Before harvest · money has run out
Raven's test score (logic puzzles, out of 10)
4.35
Attention test (Stroop, 75 items)

146 s to finish · 5.93 errors

After harvest · paid for the crop
Raven's test score (logic puzzles, out of 10)
5.45
Attention test (Stroop, 75 items)

131 s to finish · 5.16 errors

+25% higher score · 15 s faster

The gap is not explained by
  • Food: farmers did not eat less before harvest: Rs2,663 vs Rs2,592 a month on food.
  • Physical work: same result for farmers who had finished harvesting but were not yet paid.
  • Stress: heart rate and blood pressure were higher before harvest, but controlling for them does not change the result.
How large is the effect

Like losing a full night of sleep

About 13 IQ points in the laboratory study (New Jersey shoppers who thought about a $1,500 car repair). For the farmers, three quarters of that size.

Mani, A., Mullainathan, S., Shafir, E. and Zhao, J. (2013). Poverty impedes cognitive function. Science 341: 976–980. Field study pp. 978–979 and Table 1; effect size and policy pp. 979–980. 16 · Next in line

Next in line: energy subsidy reform

Once the state can pay poor households directly and accurately, it can move subsidies from energy products to people. This is the step that links DPI to the climate agenda.

DPI in place

Digital ID, data exchange (SPLP) and digital payments.

Know who needs help

Fewer inclusion and exclusion errors, updated quickly.

Compensate directly

Cash to eligible households when energy prices change.

Reform energy subsidies

Prices move closer to cost; the poor are protected.

Subsidy reforms: done and next
  • DoneKerosene → LPG, 2007 onward
  • NextLPG 3 kg: subsidise people, not the cylinder
  • NextFuel: Pertalite and diesel (solar)
  • NextElectricity: subsidised household connections
Fiscal

Spending moves from products used by everyone to the households that need it.

Climate

Energy priced closer to its cost means less waste of fossil fuel and lower emissions.

Inclusion

Poor households are compensated, so the reform does not push them into poverty.

The kerosene-to-LPG conversion (section 3) shows the gains are real. Without accurate targeting, each reform risks either hurting poor households or paying the non-poor.
17 · Climate cooperation

Why climate cooperation fails: a prisoner's dilemma

Cutting emissions costs a country now, at home. The benefit is spread over the whole world and comes later. So each country does better by letting others cut (Yusuf 2025, p. 4).

Only the climate counts

Each cell: payoff to developed | payoff to developing countries.

Both arrows point to not cutting, whatever the other side does. Both end with 1, though cutting together would give 3 each.

Development urgency counts too

Cutting also brings developing countries +2 at home: fiscal space, cleaner air, energy security.

Developing countries now gain by cutting, whatever others do. They act for their own development, as China and Vietnam did (pp. 4–5).

Climate cooperation works better when it starts from what developing countries urgently need. Energy subsidy reform, made fair by DPI, is one such case.

19 · Closing

Closing: inclusive and green development needs a strong state

DPI improves fiscal efficiency
More revenue. Linked data on people, assets, jobs and electricity use helps the state see who can pay tax.
Better spending. Help goes to people, not products: fewer errors, faster payment, less leakage.
Why the state must be strong
Markets do not fix inequality or pollution. Both are market failures. Only a government with enough fiscal capacity can correct them. A fiscally weak state cannot protect its people or its environment.
DPI makes the state stronger, so that it can protect people and the environment at the same time.
