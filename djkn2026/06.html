Three runs: baseline, moratorium, moratorium with payment

Same economy, same assumptions; only the land rule and the payment change
C · Land-cover change
SIM0 · Baseline
  • GDP growth about 6% a year
  • Labour force +2.5%, population +1.5% a year
  • Land productivity +3% a year in agriculture
  • Oil palm land +8% a year in Kalimantan and Papua, +4% East Sumatra and Sulawesi, +3% West Sumatra
  • Half of new oil palm land comes from natural forest
SIM1 · Moratorium
  • Same as the baseline until 2015
  • From 2015, new oil palm land comes only from managed forest
  • No natural forest is converted
  • Oil palm land grows half as fast
SIM2 · Moratorium + payment
  • Same as SIM1
  • Payment of US$10 per t CO2 avoided
  • Each region gets money in proportion to its emission cut
  • Paid to households, as a transfer from abroad
  • Trade balance plus transfers, as a share of GDP, held fixed

How results are read
  • Each policy run is compared with the baseline, year by year, as % deviation
  • Long run: national employment fixed, wages adjust
  • Capital moves until rates of return are back at baseline
SIM1 measures the cost of the moratorium. SIM2 asks whether a carbon payment can cover that cost.
Source: Yusuf, Roos and Horridge (2018), Asian Development Review 35(2): 85-112 (journal). Design pp. 98-101; oil palm land in 2030 p. 102.
